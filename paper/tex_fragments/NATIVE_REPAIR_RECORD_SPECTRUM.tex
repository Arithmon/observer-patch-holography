\subsection{Native stationary repair and absolute record power}
\label{subsec:native-repair-record-spectrum}

Consider a connected finite undirected port graph with positive edge attempt
rates \(w_e\), weighted Laplacian \(L\), and \(p\) ports. In a balanced
integer sector, subtract the common load floor and write
\[
 n_i\in\{0,1\},\quad \sum_i n_i=r,\quad 0<r<p,\qquad
 z=n-\frac rp\mathbf1,\quad
 \Pi=I-\frac1p\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},\quad
 \kappa=\frac{r(p-r)}{p(p-1)}.
\]
Independent Poisson attempts on edge \(e\) exchange its endpoint loads with
probability \(1/2\) and otherwise leave them unchanged. This is the fair
nearest-integer agreement rule continued after first settlement. It is a
specified observer-like system: bounded local port state, seam interaction,
readback, and records of local repair attempts. Its stationary history is a
different observable from a configuration retained at the end of its first
settling sweep.

\begin{theorem}[Stationary density and occupation records]
\label{thm:native-stationary-history}
The uniform law \(\pi\) on the \(r\)-subsets is the unique stationary law.
The process is reversible, its configuration generator \(G\) obeys
\(Gz=-Lz/2\), and in stationarity
\[
 \mathbb E_\pi[zz^{\mathsf T}]=\kappa\Pi,\qquad
 \mathbb E_\pi[z(t)z(0)^{\mathsf T}]
   =\kappa e^{-|t|L/2}\Pi.
\]
For each fixed matrix \(B\), at fixed graph and from any initial law,
\[
 T^{-1/2}\int_0^T Bz(t)\,dt
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal N(0,4\kappa B L^+B^{\mathsf T}).
\]
For the literal time average \(f_T=T^{-1}\int_0^Tz(t)\,dt\), its
stationary variance along a Euclidean-unit eigenvector of positive
eigenvalue \(\lambda\) is instead
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(v^{\mathsf T}f_T)=\kappa h(\lambda T/2),\qquad
 h(u)=\frac{2(u-1+e^{-u})}{u^2},\quad h(0)=1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each configuration swap has the same reverse rate. Connectedness makes the
fixed-occupancy chain irreducible, giving uniform detailed balance and
uniqueness. Sampling without replacement gives the displayed equal-time
covariance. The mean swap increment on an oriented edge with incidence
vector \(b_e\) is \(-b_eb_e^{\mathsf T}z/2\); summing rates proves the
generator identity, and its semigroup gives the unequal-time covariance.
On the mean-zero subspace the Poisson solution is \(H=2L^+z\), with
\(GH=-z\). The time integral is its Dynkin martingale plus a bounded
endpoint term. Ergodicity identifies the martingale bracket and bounded
jumps imply its central limit condition; the bracket is equivalently
\(2\int_0^\infty\kappa e^{-tL/2}\Pi\,dt=4\kappa L^+\).
Finally, integrating \(\kappa e^{-\lambda|t-s|/2}\) over the observation
square and dividing by \(T^2\) gives \(h\).
\end{proof}

This specializes the finite reversible additive-functional limit
\cite{KipnisVaradhanNative} to the actual repair rule. It does not assert a joint refinement and long-time
limit. The sector, edge rates, elapsed-time convention, memory duration and
record gain are declared. In particular, the two normalizations above
differ by \(\sqrt T\); their powers differ by \(T\). A change of time
units rescales rates inversely and leaves \(\lambda T\) unchanged.

A specified leaky local record gives a bounded-amplitude alternative:
\[
 \dot y_i=z_i-\gamma y_i,\qquad R_\gamma=\sqrt{2\gamma}\,y,
 \qquad \operatorname{Cov}(R_\gamma)
   =4\kappa(L+2\gamma I)^{-1}\Pi,\quad \gamma>0.
\]
In joint stationarity, \(y_i(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\gamma s}z_i(t-s)\,ds\).
Integrating the same unequal-time covariance proves the formula, and
\(|y_i|\le\max(r/p,1-r/p)/\gamma\). This is a covariance identity;
the finite-leak field need not be Gaussian. The leak, output gain and
stationary preparation are specified, and finite-precision storage or
saturation requires a separate implementation bound.

\begin{theorem}[Nonlinear readout projection at every finite window]
For a centered vector-valued current-state readout \(q\), set
\[
 B=\kappa^{-1}\mathbb E_\pi[qz^{\mathsf T}],\qquad q=Bz+g.
\]
Then \(\mathbb E_\pi[gz^{\mathsf T}]=0\). Writing \(C_T(q)\) for the
stationary covariance of its time average and \(S=-G\),
\[
 C_T(q)=\langle q,h(TS)q^{\mathsf T}\rangle_\pi
       =B C_T(z) B^{\mathsf T}+C_T(g),\qquad C_T(g)\succeq0.
\]
The asymptotic fluctuation covariance is
\(4\kappa BL^+B^{\mathsf T}+2\langle g,S^+g^{\mathsf T}\rangle_\pi\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The components of \(z\) span a \(G\)-invariant subspace. Self-adjointness
in \(L^2(\pi)\) makes its orthogonal complement invariant too, so the
cross correlations vanish at every time lag. Spectral integration of the
observation square gives \(h(TS)\); it is positive. The inverse-generator
formula follows by integrating the decaying semigroup on centered functions.
\end{proof}

On all \(\binom{12}{6}=924\) states of the half-filled production carrier,
\(\kappa=3/11\) and the positive graph eigenvalues are
\(5-\sqrt5,6,5+\sqrt5\), with multiplicities \(3,5,3\).
These are graph bands. At unit attempt-rate normalization the port-average
root mean square of \(f_T\) is respectively
\(0.497739,0.478409,0.352867,0.141920,0.0459402\) at
\(T=0.01,0.1,1,10,100\). Independent moment evolution checks the
absolute finite-window formula for load, local Laplacian drive and a
centered two-port product; the product has a nonzero orthogonal remainder.
No geometric volume law is assigned to these test readouts.

\subsection{Locally sampled attempt records}
\label{subsec:native-event-records}
Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)\), where
\(d_i=\sum_{e\ni i}w_e\), and let \(D_e\) have diagonal entries
\(1/d_i\) at the endpoints of \(e\), zero elsewhere. At \emph{every}
attempt, including null attempts, append the pre-attempt vector \(D_ez\).
Let \(A_T\) be its accumulated value on a common elapsed-time slice.
Each port uses its local load, calibrated local attempt rate and the
prepared filling \(r/p\).

\begin{theorem}[Attempt-record covariance]
At fixed graph, from any initial law,
\[
 T^{-1/2}A_T\Longrightarrow\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{\rm ev}),\qquad
 \Sigma_{\rm ev}=4\kappa L^+
 -\kappa(\Pi D^{-1}+D^{-1}\Pi)+J,
 \quad J=\kappa\sum_e w_eD_e\Pi D_e.
\]
The correction satisfies
\[
 \|\Sigma_{\rm ev}-4\kappa L^+\|\le\frac{3\kappa}{d_{\min}},
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{\rm ev}v}{4\kappa/\lambda}-1\right|
 \le\frac{3\lambda}{4d_{\min}}
\]
for any mean-zero unit eigenvector \(Lv=\lambda v\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The conditional reward rate is \(\sum_e w_eD_ez=z\). With
\(H=2L^+z\), reward plus \(\Delta H\) is a martingale increment.
The bounded endpoint correction has no effect on the limit. Its stationary
bracket contains the occupation contribution \(4\kappa L^+\), reward
square \(J\), and cross bracket
\(-\kappa L^+LD^{-1}=-\kappa\Pi D^{-1}\) plus its transpose.
Here \(b_eb_e^{\mathsf T}D_e=b_eb_e^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}\).
Since \(0\preceq\Pi\preceq I\), one has
\(0\preceq J\preceq\kappa D^{-1}\). The triangle inequality gives
the operator bound and its Rayleigh restriction gives the relative bound.
Finite state space and bounded event marks justify the martingale limit.
\end{proof}

The counting correction is correlated with subsequent repair. Treating it
as independent noise is incorrect: the two-port control gives one half
of the occupation-record variance. Recording successful moves only,
subtracting a deterministic mean from raw uncentered marks, or stopping
each patch after its own fixed event count defines a different observable.
The asymptotic accumulator is unbounded; finite memory requires its own
storage, reset or saturation law. These qualifications matter when
attaching the record to a physical observer clock.

\subsection{Temporal filtering and fixed-area normalization}
\label{subsec:native-filter-boundary}
For a common deterministic real kernel \(w\in L^2([0,\infty))\), extended
by zero, let \(\widehat w\) be its Fourier transform. The stationary
filtered density mode of decay rate \(a=\lambda/2\) has variance
\[
 V(\lambda)=\frac\kappa\pi\int_{\mathbb R}
       |\widehat w(\omega)|^2\frac a{a^2+\omega^2}\,d\omega.
\]
Consequently \(\lambda V(\lambda)\) is nondecreasing and, for a nonzero
kernel, \(-1\le d\log V/d\log\lambda\le1\). Indeed,
\(a^2/(a^2+\omega^2)\) is nondecreasing in \(a>0\); the logarithmic
derivative of the Lorentzian lies between \(-1\) and \(1\), and the
integrated derivative is its positive weighted average. Domination on
compact positive \(a\)-intervals justifies differentiation. Under a
separately established three-dimensional metric identification
\(\lambda\propto k^2\), this common temporal filter cannot generate
power redder than \(k^{-2}\). It therefore does not supply \(k^{-3}\)
bulk curvature power. This statement excludes nonlinear, spatially varying,
adaptive and nonstationary readouts from its scope.

There is also a normalization bound independent of spherical quadrature.
For arbitrary fixed port directions \(\hat n_i\), define empirical harmonic
coefficients with weights \(4\pi/p\). If the same deterministic,
trajectory-independent temporal kernel has \(L^1\) gain at most \(W\),
then the ensemble mean harmonic pseudo-power obeys
\[
 \widetilde C_\ell:=\frac1{2\ell+1}\sum_m\mathbb E|a_{\ell m}|^2
 \le\frac{4\pi\kappa W^2}{p}
                  \le\frac{\pi W^2}{p-1}.
\]
To see this, stationary covariance and semigroup contraction bound every
coefficient variance by \(\kappa W^2\|\Pi\alpha_{\ell m}\|^2\).
The spherical-harmonic addition theorem gives
\((2\ell+1)^{-1}\sum_m\|\alpha_{\ell m}\|^2=4\pi/p\), even for
irregular or repeated directions. Ordinary averages have \(W=1\), so
their fixed-area expected pseudo-power vanishes on refinement. This is
not a pointwise bound on every realized record. The amplified
\(T^{-1/2}\) history has growing gain and does not satisfy a uniform
\(W\) premise. This is a restriction on the specified load record,
not a theorem that geometric curvature vanishes.
