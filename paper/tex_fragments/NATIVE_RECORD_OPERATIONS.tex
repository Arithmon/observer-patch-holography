\subsection{Native scalar records and the selection boundary}
\label{sec:native-record-operations}

Consider bounded self-reading patches whose scalar ports undergo pair
averaging, with local readback, retained records and a declared controller.
The following constructions distinguish operations available in that grammar
from selection of the controller and its observer interface.

\paragraph{Passive records and reset.}
On a finite connected support, every state-only function invariant under
each allowed pair mean on all nonnegative inputs factors through total load on those inputs.
No continuity or linearity assumption is needed. Indeed, moving a nonnegative
addend between adjacent coordinates gives two states with the same mean
image. Invariance identifies their readouts. Moving each addend along a
path to a fixed root proves the factorization. This equal-image argument
does not execute inverse repairs. Restricted encodings and recorded
histories are outside its quantifiers.

A positive raw port initially equal to $a$ retains at least $a/2^m$ after
$m$ incident means with nonnegative ancillary loads. Fresh zero neighbours
attain this bound and retain the removed load. Thus finite raw-zero reset
is impossible in this class. The exact identity
\[
 \sum_i x_i^2-\sum_i (P_{uv}x)_i^2=(x_u-x_v)^2/2
\]
also excludes an exact closed cycle of the whole register state that contains
a nontrivial mean, and a full-amplitude copy of a nonzero two-rail balanced
record, with the original intact, while restoring every ancillary coordinate. This quadratic functional
has no physical-energy identification. The proofs are in
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceNonlinearRecord.lean},
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourcePassiveReset.lean} and
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourcePassiveMemoryBudget.lean}.

\paragraph{Encoded copying and reusable reads.}
Encode a logical sign by $(b+a,b-a)$, with $b\geq |a|$, and prepare a blank
pair $(b,b)$. Averaging corresponding rails produces two pairs with
amplitude $a/2$; averaging either pair clears its encoded amplitude.
Repeated copy-and-clear transport costs $3n$ means and attenuates the signal
to $a/2^n$. For positive amplitude $a$, if initial sup-norm error is at most
$E_0$, every rail is disturbed by at most $\delta$ after each mean, and
readout error is at most $\rho$, the sufficient sign condition is
\[
 E_0+3n\delta+\rho<a/2^n;
\]
the opposite sign follows by exchanging rails. Nonexpansiveness of pair averaging proves the accumulated-error bound.
The construction supplies neither amplitude restoration nor an exact
unit-gain analog archive. Its proofs and independent execution replay are
in \path{Lean/Geometry/SourceEncodedMemory.lean} and
\path{code/source_encoded_memory/}.

On twelve ports of a captured 15,360-port support, two prepared records can be read in the
order $A,B,A$ through a four-cell paired-rail bus. All 16 declared input
pairs retain their complete histories, totaling 27,264 means. Raw residual
influence survives cleanup; the finite codebook and positive error margins
under supplied preparation, rounding, disturbance and readout allowances
certify the decoded records. A seven-mean extension writes an encoded sum $C=A+B$ at one quarter of the
input amplitude while halving the old records
(\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceNativeRecords.lean}), and reads $C,A,C,B$ with
the record scales tracked explicitly. Independent replay covers all 36,464 means over its 16 input pairs.
The smallest certified read margin exceeds $0.0032996$ under the stated
preparation, rounding, disturbance and readout budgets. The complete
controllers, supported seams, consumed writers and precision controls are
specified in \path{code/source_reusable_bus/} and
\path{code/source_native_updates/}.

For a paired-rail chain of $n\geq1$ edges with amplitudes initially bounded by
$A\geq0$, clear its first pair and sweep the adjacent rail copies forward.
After $(n+1)^2 k$ sweeps the maximum residual is at most
\[
 A\frac{(n+1)^2}{2n+1}\,2^{-k},
\]
at a cost of $(1+2n)(n+1)^2 k$ scalar means; archives outside the chain are
untouched. A weighted sup-norm estimate with weights
$(n+1)^2-(n-i)^2$ gives one-sweep contraction at most
$1-(n+1)^{-2}$, and blocks of $(n+1)^2$ sweeps give the displayed bound.
The zero-edge case is exact clearing. Forward read attenuation is a separate
cost. \path{Lean/Geometry/SourceBusScaling.lean} proves these statements for an
abstract paired-rail chain; the rail embedding into a captured support, the
preparation and the schedule are supplied.

\paragraph{Reusable accumulation from a retained unit.}
Represent an integer input $v_i$ by the amplitude
$a_i=g v_i/2^{e_i}$ on rails $(b+a_i,b-a_i)$, with $g>0$ and
nonnegative integer scales $e_i$. The baseline can be chosen so that the
prepared rails are nonnegative. Reserve record $0$ as a mutable accumulator,
record $1$ as workspace and record $2$ as a unit input $v_2=1$;
reserve additional blank bus records for the final read.
The other input values are supplied once. All subsequent data writes in the
following construction are scalar pair means; the requests and scale
metadata are supplied controller data.

Four means across the accumulator--workspace rectangle clear both encoded
amplitudes: first average corresponding rails, then average the two opposite
rail pairs. This identity holds for arbitrary initial amplitudes with a
common baseline. Two more means copy the unit into the cleared accumulator.
The unit and accumulator amplitudes both halve, and their updated scales
represent the same logical value $1$. The previous accumulator value is
retired; it is not archived by this reset.

For an input $s\geq2$, set $t=\max(e_0,e_s+1)$. Halve the accumulator
$t-e_0$ times and input $s$ another $t-e_s-1$ times, using the three-mean
copy-and-clear construction. Then copy $s$ into the blank workspace,
copy the accumulator into that workspace and clear the workspace. These
last five scalar means leave
\[
 a_0'=\frac{g(v_0+v_s)}{2^{t+1}},\qquad
 a_s'=\frac{g v_s}{2^t},\qquad a_1'=0.
\]
Every other input amplitude is unchanged. Thus finite induction over the
actual concatenated words preserves the original logical input values,
including under repeated requests, and gives the final episode value
$1+\sum_{s\in\mathcal R}v_s$, with multiplicity in the supplied request
list $\mathcal R$. For $S\geq1$ episodes containing $N$ additions and
initial scales at most $M$, the proved bounds are
\[
 e_i\leq M+S+2N,\qquad
 W\leq6S+N\bigl[8+3(M+S+2N)\bigr].
\]
Here $W$ counts all scalar means before the final read. A prepared
four-edge paired bus adds eight means and four to the receiver's decoding
scale. It attenuates the final accumulator amplitude by $1/16$.

Let $p,m$ be the final local rail samples. Preparation error $E_0$,
per-mean sup-norm disturbance $\delta$ and sample error $\rho$ give
\[
 \left|\frac{p-m}{2}-\frac{g v}{2^e}\right|
 \leq E_0+W\delta+\rho.
\]
In this inequality $W$ includes the final transport. Clearing a record
does not restart the error count. Normalize the observation by $2^e/g$.
For a public magnitude bound $B$ obtained from the input caps, intersect its
closed error interval with $[-B,B]\cap\mathbb Z$. The clipped ceiling and
floor endpoints agree exactly when this set contains one integer; the
decoder then publishes that integer and otherwise abstains. The actual
sum is a feasible candidate by the transport bound and the triangle
inequality for its input caps. A normalized radius below $1/2$ therefore
proves publication of the sum, rather than assuming the desired answer.
The decoder uses only the two samples, the scale, the cumulative mean count
and the public cap.

The captured realization prepares fourteen scalar ports with $b=2$,
$Q=2^{256}$ and $g=(Q-3)/Q$. Under the declared allowances
$E_0=\rho=1/(4Q)$ and $\delta=5/(8Q)$, the normalized radius is
\[
 R=\frac{(4+5W)2^e}{8(Q-3)}.
\]
Thus $(4+5W)2^e<4(Q-3)$ suffices. Exact replay covers 1,280 histories
and 78,255 means, including five histories of fifteen episodes and 108
additions each. It checks supported seams, consumed values and current
writers, 12,182 odd-sum rounding cases and 67,720 scalar samples; the
largest decoding scale is $104$. The full tapes are regenerated from the
compact evidence in \path{code/source_native_accumulator/}.
The arbitrary-finite-word, error, cost and exact decoder theorems are in
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceNativeAccumulator.lean},
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceAccumulatorProgram.lean} and
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceAccumulatorDecoder.lean}. Logical input retention in this accumulator
does not give immutable amplitudes or committed layer archives. Conditional
stored computation with two protected banks is established in
Section~\ref{sec:native-stored-programs}. The bound
charges native mean work, with controller computation, metadata, physical
isolation and a physical publication instrument supplied separately.

\paragraph{Conditional publication and stopped histories.}
For any declared observation model, associate an observation with its set
of compatible payloads. On feasible observations, every sound deterministic
publication must identify a singleton set. Publishing that unique value
is maximal among sound policies on feasible observations. The implemented
policy abstains on ambiguous sets and on empty sets, where soundness alone
would be vacuous.
This derives a policy relative to an observation model, not a physical
instrument or a requirement that an observer execute it.

For a positive source amplitude $A$, blank other records and $t$ paired
copies, let $E_t=E_0+2t\delta+\rho$. The ideal unsigned receiver amplitude
lies in $[0,A]$; when nonzero it is at least $A/2^t$. The two signed
payloads therefore admit the respective observation intervals
\[
 I_+=[-E_t,A+E_t],\qquad I_-=[-A-E_t,E_t].
\]
Their singleton policy publishes the positive sign only for
$E_t<z\leq A+E_t$, the negative sign only for
$-A-E_t\leq z<-E_t$, and otherwise abstains. This policy is maximal for
these declared gain-and-error intervals, which need not equal the discrete
set of observations of a particular word family. It is sound for every
native word under the error assumptions. Once influence reaches the
receiver, the strict margin $2E_t<A/2^t$ proves availability as well.
Words that avoid the source cut can leave the receiver uninformative while
incurring their full update cost.

On a path of $d\geq1$ paired edges, nonzero influence occupies a prefix;
exactly one of the $d$ edge letters advances an unfinished prefix. Under
the supplied uniform law on all $d^H$ complete words of horizon $H$, let
$T$ be the first checkpoint with influence at the receiver. Then
\[
 \Pr(T>H)=d^{-H}\sum_{j=0}^{\min(d-1,H)}
              {H\choose j}(d-1)^{H-j},\qquad
 \mathbb E[\text{scalar means}]
       =2\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\Pr(T>t).
\]
The convention $0^0=1$ includes $d=1$. With a sufficient margin at the
declared horizon, the implemented stopping time is $\min(T,H)$.
A first arrival at $t$ retains the probability mass of its $d^{H-t}$
complete-word suffixes, although those suffixes are not executed. Failed
attempts and deadline aborts are included; the expectation is not
conditioned on publication. Uniform one-time marginals alone do not imply
this complete-word law.

For four edges, $Q=2^{14}$ and source amplitude $(Q-3)/Q$, exhaustive
replay through horizon eight checks 174,762 word--payload cases and
2,642,144 means. At that horizon publication has probability
$7459/65536$, abort has probability $58077/65536$, and the unconditional
expected mean count is $32245/2048$. Preparation, update and sample
allowances are the same grid-scaled bounds used above. Finite precision
does not establish indefinitely repeated availability.
The general singleton, native error, frontier and complete-history
counting recurrences are proved in
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceReadAcceptance.lean} and
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceReadAcceptanceSchedule.lean}. The binomial
closed form is an analytic consequence of those recurrences, independently
checked for the finite cases in \path{code/source_read_acceptance/}.

\paragraph{Selection of successful histories.}
Let $K$ be a convex family of nonnegative finite vectors, with strictly
positive reference coordinates and entropy weights. If the weighted
relative entropy attains its minimum at $p_*$, then, as for classical
information projections, every coordinate positive in any feasible $q$ is
positive in $p_*$. Mixing a purported zero
coordinate with $q$ produces a negative $t\log t$ term that dominates the
change of first order in $t$. Consequently a
nonnegative failure observable of the scored atoms vanishes at the minimum
only if it vanishes throughout $K$. Applied with unit weights to complete repair histories,
a guaranteed remote read requires every feasible schedule to exclude words
that avoid the source cut: such words leave the exterior transcript
identical under different source payloads.

This criterion concerns the atoms actually scored. An injective observer
cover is insufficient to transfer it to every history event. For
$p=(t,1/2,1/2-t)$, $0\leq t\leq1/2$, and reference
$r=(1/4,1/4,1/2)$, full-history relative entropy has unique minimizer
$(1/6,1/2,1/3)$. Scoring the cover $(p_0+p_1,p_2)$ against its pushed-forward
reference instead gives $(0,1/2,1/2)$. Both covers determine the state on
this family, but reconstruction of $p_0$ from the coarse cover subtracts
$1/2$ and is not a nonnegative readout. The support theorem, both minima
and the native cut argument are proved in
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceConstrainedSelection.lean},
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceSelectionControls.lean} and
\path{Lean/Geometry/SourceConstrainedRead.lean}; exact controls are in
\path{code/source_read_selection/}.

These results neither select the feasible history grammar, reference, cover
and stopping policy nor derive the complete immutable scalar-read service.
The finite classical controllers supply no quantum instrument, physical
precision, clock calibration or count-volume interpretation of their
additional operations.
