\paragraph{Complete radial-lift theorem packet.}
\label{par:oph330-complete-radial-lift}
The screen-to-radial map is a forward restriction with a nonunique unrestricted inverse.
For a flat source window $W$ and geometric normalization $Z_q$,
\[
 C_{\ell,W}^q
 =4\pi Z_q^2\int_0^\infty d\ln k\,
 \Delta_\zeta^2(k)|\Psi_{\ell,W}(k)|^2,
 \qquad
 \Psi_{\ell,W}(k)=\int W(dr)j_\ell(kr).
\]
The packet below certifies sufficient uniqueness routes through radial dilation,
radial tomography and common-bulk consistency on unbounded radii.
They are not asserted to be exhaustive or mutually
exclusive. A source-derived parametric or dynamical law, an injective
source-restricted basis, analytic or PDE constraints, or another fully typed
orbit-separating construction remains admissible. A finite prior continuation
has the type \textsc{ConditionalRadialContinuation}.

\textbf{Theorem (one-shell correlation restriction and exact no-go).}
\label{thm:oph330-shell-correlation-restriction}
For a thin shell of radius $R$, the complete angular covariance satisfies
\[
 K_R(\mu)
 =Z_q^2\xi_\zeta\!\left(R\sqrt{2-2\mu}\right)
 =\sum_{\ell\ge0}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}C_{\ell,R}^qP_\ell(\mu).
\]
Hence even the noiseless sequence $\{C_{\ell,R}^q\}_{\ell\ge0}$ determines the
three-dimensional correlation only on $0\le s\le2R$.  The full one-shell
operator has an infinite-dimensional kernel, including positivity-preserving
ambiguities.  Explicitly, for any nonzero
$g\in C_c^\infty((2R,\infty))$,
\[
 h_g(k)=\frac{2k^3}{\pi}\int_0^\infty r^2g(r)j_0(kr)\,dr
\]
has correlation perturbation $\xi_{h_g}=g$ and therefore changes no shell
multipole.  Choosing $B=|h_g|+w$ with any strictly positive integrable $w$ makes
$B$ and $B+h_g$ two distinct positive spectra with the same complete shell
covariance.

\textit{Proof.}
The spherical-Bessel addition theorem gives the displayed restriction identity.
The map $\mu\mapsto R\sqrt{2-2\mu}$ covers exactly $[0,2R]$.  Radial Fourier
inversion gives $\xi_{h_g}=g$ and is injective, so the kernel contains an
infinite-dimensional copy of $C_c^\infty((2R,\infty))$.  The positive pair differs
by the same null element.  \hfill$\square$

\textbf{Corollary (exact low-mode-removed kernel).}
\label{cor:oph330-low-mode-shell-kernel}
For a continuous signed correlation perturbation \(\xi_h\), invisibility to
every shell multipole \(\ell\ge0\) is equivalent to
\(\xi_h(s)=0\) on \(0\le s\le2R\). Invisibility to every retained OPH
multipole \(\ell\ge2\) is equivalent to
\[
\xi_h(s)=a+b\left(1-\frac{s^2}{2R^2}\right),
\qquad 0\le s\le2R,
\]
for constants \(a,b\). Legendre completeness gives the first statement.
Removing \(\ell=0,1\) leaves the span of \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) invisible, which
gives the second.


\textbf{Theorem (one common bulk on unbounded round radii).}
\label{thm:common-bulk-unbounded-radii}
Assume one homogeneous isotropic generalized field on a common Euclidean
three-dimensional time slice, with well-defined spherical restrictions.
Its covariance is represented off the diagonal by a locally integrable
radial function \(\xi(s)\), with no additional contact distributions.
Suppose an unbounded sequence \(R_j\) of round radii has
\[
 C_\ell(R_j)=\frac{2\pi A_j}{\ell(\ell+1)},\qquad
 \ell\ge1,\quad A_j>0,
\]
in one common readout normalization. Then all \(A_j=A\), and
\[
 \xi(s)=-A\log s+c\quad\text{for almost every }s>0,\qquad
 P_3(k)=\frac{2\pi^2A}{k^3}\quad(k>0).
\]
The ideal field is understood modulo constants, on smooth mean-zero test
observables; it does not have finite point variance. The covariance
identity is distributional, with the displayed logarithm as a chosen
off-diagonal representative. Gaussianity is not
needed for the covariance conclusion. If the hypotheses concern only
\(\ell\ge2\), the residual freedom is \(c+ds^2\), representing
unobserved zero-wave-number or affine data.

\textit{Proof.}
The identity \(\int_0^\infty j_\ell(x)^2\,dx/x
=1/[2\ell(\ell+1)]\), or Legendre expansion of the logarithm, identifies
each mean-zero spherical kernel. Chord restriction and completeness give
\(\xi(s)=-A_j\log s+c_j\) almost everywhere on \(0<s<2R_j\).
Every two intervals overlap. Linear independence of \(\log s\) and
\(1\) forces their coefficients to agree. Unbounded radii cover all
positive separations; radial Fourier uniqueness gives the stated spectrum
away from zero. With the dipole removed, use independence of
\(\log s,1,s^2\) instead. Contact terms are excluded by hypothesis,
so off-diagonal equality does not hide an extra diagonal distribution.
\hfill\(\square\)

Separate screen marginals do not establish the common-bulk premise. The
metrics, time slice, normalization and cross-screen records must be jointly
compatible. For finitely many radii, unobserved separations allow
the preceding null-space perturbations; a finite run cannot invoke
unbounded coverage.

\textbf{Example (positive alternatives to the scale-invariant baseline).}
For a unit-radius screen set
\[
 h(s)=(s-3)^2(4-s)^2\mathbf1_{[3,4]}(s),\qquad
 H(k)=4\pi\int_3^4s^2h(s)\frac{\sin(ks)}{ks}\,ds.
\]
Writing \(g(s)=s h(s)\), both \(g\) and \(g'\) vanish at the support
endpoints. Two integrations by parts and
\(\int_3^4|g''(s)|\,ds\le30\) imply
\( |k^3H(k)|\le120\pi\). Hence
\[
 P_\pm(k)=\frac{2\pi^2A}{k^3}\pm\frac{\pi A}{120}H(k)
 \ \ge\ \frac{\pi^2A}{k^3}>0.
\]
Both positive generalized Gaussian covariances restrict to exactly the
same law as the baseline on the unit sphere, since \(h=0\) for all its
chord separations. Their bulk covariances differ. This exhibits ambiguity
around the scale-invariant baseline itself, without permitting a negative
spectrum. The analytic bound proves positivity for all \(k\); numerical
quadrature is only a check. The source-pinned radial controls under
\path{evidence/observer_dynamics_20260925/sim-analysis/codex/necessity/boundary_bulk/}
check the forward integrals and this perturbation with analytic tail
bounds, not formal interval arithmetic.

\textbf{Producer theorem (source-refinement orbit to physical dilation).}
\label{thm:oph330-source-orbit-transfer}
Let $\mathcal H^{\rm src}_r$ be the scale-labelled scalar mode space generated by
the cofinal refinement orbit of the released screen, including its scale label.
A single angular cut is insufficient. Let $U_r$ map the orbit unitarily onto the physical rank-one adiabatic source
subspace.  If
\[
 D_{s,r}U_r=U_rR_{s,r},\qquad
 C_{\zeta,r}=U_rC_{{\rm src},r}U_r^*,
\]
then
\[
 D_{s,r}^{-1}C_{\zeta,r}D_{s,r}-u_r(s)C_{\zeta,r}
 =U_r\left(R_{s,r}^{-1}C_{{\rm src},r}R_{s,r}
           -u_r(s)C_{{\rm src},r}\right)U_r^*.
\]
Thus the source and physical operator residuals have equal norm. For a
bounded-operator limit on a common Hilbert space, strong covariance convergence
with a uniform norm bound, strong convergence of the dilation maps and their
inverses, $u_r(s)\to u(s)$, and vanishing norm residual imply
$D_s^{-1}C_\zeta D_s=u(s)C_\zeta$ in the continuum.
This sufficient criterion does not cover a nonzero tilted power law on all
$0<k<\infty$: unitary conjugation preserves the norm of a bounded operator,
so $\|C_\zeta\|=|u(s)|\|C_\zeta\|$ would force $u(s)=1$ for positive $u(s)$
and $C_\zeta\ne0$.

For the global power-law branch, use instead a local limit receipt. After
embedding on the common physical mode space, let $\mathcal D$ be the dense
space of $L^2$ functions supported in compact $\ln k$ bands, and let $D_s$ be
the exact physical dilation, preserving $\mathcal D$. Require
$C_{\zeta,r}f\to C_\zeta f$ for every $f\in\mathcal D$ and
\[
 \bigl(D_s^{-1}C_{\zeta,r}D_s-u_r(s)C_{\zeta,r}\bigr)f\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(f\in\mathcal D).
\]
If finite dilation maps are used to establish the latter condition, their
replacement by $D_s$ must also have vanishing error on this core. The resulting
identity holds on $\mathcal D$, with a possibly unbounded covariance operator;
it does not assert a global uniform covariance norm bound.
The commuting square states that source refinement followed by the physical
embedding agrees with physical embedding followed by wavelength dilation, and
it transfers the source covariance error exactly to the physical covariance.

\textit{Proof.}
Conjugate the source covariance by $U_r$ and use the commuting square; unitary
invariance gives the finite residual identity. For the bounded limit, strong
convergence and uniform boundedness allow passage through the products. For
the local limit, both $f$ and $D_sf$ belong to $\mathcal D$, so pass to the
limit in each of the two terms using the fixed bounded unitary $D_s^{-1}$.
\hfill$\square$

\textbf{Theorem (source-dilation uniqueness).}
\label{thm:oph330-source-dilation-uniqueness}
Let $\Delta_\zeta^2$ be a nonnegative measurable radial function, locally
essentially bounded in $\ln k$, and nonzero on a set of positive measure.
Let $C_\zeta=M_{\Delta_\zeta^2}$ on the common physical
$L^2(d\ln k\,d\Omega_k)$ mode basis, with its natural multiplication-operator
domain. The operator may be unbounded; the dilation identity below is required
on the common compact-band core $\mathcal D$. Define
\[
 (D_sf)(k,\hat k)=f(e^{-s}k,\hat k).
\]
If the source refinement packet proves, for all real $s,t$ and positive
$u_q(s)$,
\[
 D_s^{-1}C_\zeta D_s=u_q(s)C_\zeta,
 \qquad
 u_q(s+t)=u_q(s)u_q(t),
 \qquad
 -\left.\frac{d}{ds}\ln u_q(s)\right|_{s=0}=\theta,
\]
then $u_q(s)=e^{-\theta s}$ and there is $A>0$ such that
\[
 \Delta_\zeta^2(k)=Ak^{-\theta}\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
\]
For a continuous source representative and pivot $k_\star$,
\[
 \boxed{\Delta_\zeta^2(k)=A_\zeta(k/k_\star)^{-\theta}},
 \qquad A_\zeta=\Delta_\zeta^2(k_\star).
\]

\textit{Proof.}
The stated logarithmic derivative at zero gives continuity there and solves
the positive multiplicative Cauchy equation for $u_q$.
Conjugation of the multiplication operator gives
$\Delta_\zeta^2(e^sk)=e^{-\theta s}\Delta_\zeta^2(k)$ almost everywhere for
each $s$.  In logarithmic coordinates,
$e^{\theta t}\Delta_\zeta^2(e^t)$ is invariant under all rational translations
on one common full-measure set; bounded-transform convolution makes it constant
almost everywhere. Nonnegativity and the nonzero premise make that constant
strictly positive. Continuity upgrades the identity to every $k$.
The compact-band functions form a core of the stated locally bounded
multiplication operator, so the identity also identifies its natural domains.
\hfill$\square$

\textbf{Theorem (exact flat thin-shell lift).}
\label{thm:oph330-exact-flat-thin-shell-lift}
On the source-dilation branch, for $-2<\theta<4$ and $\ell\ge2$,
\[
 \int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{x}x^{-\theta}j_\ell^2(x)
 =\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4}
  \frac{\Gamma(1+\theta/2)}{\Gamma(3/2+\theta/2)}
  \frac{\Gamma(\ell-\theta/2)}{\Gamma(\ell+2+\theta/2)},
\]
so
\[
 C_{\ell,R_\star}^q
 =A_q\frac{\Gamma(\ell-\theta/2)}
            {\Gamma(\ell+2+\theta/2)},
\]
with the complete amplitude conversion
\[
 \boxed{
 A_\zeta
 =\frac{A_q}{\pi^{3/2}Z_q^2(k_\star R_\star)^\theta}
  \frac{\Gamma(3/2+\theta/2)}{\Gamma(1+\theta/2)}.}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Insert the source power law into the exact forward map, set $x=kR_\star$, and
apply the Weber--Schafheitlin square integral.  The convergence strip follows
from $j_\ell(x)\sim x^\ell/(2\ell+1)!!$ at zero and $j_\ell(x)=O(x^{-1})$ at
infinity.  \hfill$\square$

At the source-native pivot $k_\star R_\star=1$ with $Z_q=1$ and the
source-law tilt $\theta$, the conversion factor evaluates to approximately
$0.16080676$.  It is an arithmetic consequence of the symbolic relation and
supplies no source amplitude by itself.

\label{cor:oph330-source-family-injectivity}

\textbf{Theorem (finite-window bound).}
\label{thm:oph330-finite-window-bound}
Let $\ell\ge1$ be an integer, $R_\star,k_\star>0$, $A_\zeta>0$, and let
$W$ be a nonnegative probability measure on positive radii with finite
$\theta/2$ moment. For $0<\theta<2\ell$, define
\[
 I_\ell(\theta)=\int d\ln x\,x^{-\theta}j_\ell^2(x),
\quad
 J_\ell(\theta)=I_\ell(\theta-2)
 -\left[\ell(\ell+1)-\frac{\theta(\theta+1)}2\right]I_\ell(\theta),
\]
or, equivalently by gamma recurrence,
\[
 J_\ell(\theta)=I_\ell(\theta)
 \left[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\theta}
       +\frac{(\theta+1)(\theta-2)}4\right].
\]
\[
 \eta_{\ell,W}
 =\frac{2\sqrt{J_\ell(\theta)}}{\theta}
  \int W(dr)|r^{\theta/2}-R_\star^{\theta/2}|.
\]
Then
\[
 |C_{\ell,W}^q-C_{\ell,R_\star}^q|
 \le4\pi Z_q^2A_\zeta k_\star^\theta\eta_{\ell,W}
 \left(2R_\star^{\theta/2}\sqrt{I_\ell(\theta)}+\eta_{\ell,W}\right).
\]

\textit{Proof.}
In the Hilbert norm
$\|f\|_\theta^2=\int d\ln k\,k^{-\theta}|f(k)|^2$,
$\|j_\ell(kr)\|_\theta=r^{\theta/2}\sqrt{I_\ell}$ and
$\|\partial_rj_\ell(kr)\|_\theta=r^{\theta/2-1}\sqrt{J_\ell}$.
The fundamental theorem of calculus, Minkowski, and
$|\|f\|^2-\|g\|^2|\le\|f-g\|(\|f\|+\|g\|)$ give the bound.  \hfill$\square$

Normalization and positivity of $W$ are premises: for example,
$W=2\delta_{R_\star}$ gives $\eta_{\ell,W}=0$ while multiplying the shell
spectrum by four. A signed window would require its total variation in the
norm estimate.

The reference implementation evaluates this analytic bound in binary64; it
does not provide an outward-rounded interval certificate. Near $\theta=0$ it
uses the recurrence form of $J_\ell$ and evaluates the radius difference as
$R_\star^{\theta/2}\operatorname{expm1}[(\theta/2)\ln(r/R_\star)]$.
Direct subtraction of rounded powers can incorrectly return a zero bound for
a nontrivial window. Numerical promotion requires a separate rounding-error
budget in addition to the exact inequality.

\textbf{Theorem (finite singular-value and prior continuation).}
\label{thm:oph330-finite-prior-continuation}
For a declared finite radial basis, write \(C=Ap\). If
\(A\in\mathbb R^{N_\ell\times N_k}\) has rank \(r\), then
\(\dim\ker A=N_k-r\), and every exact solution is
\(p=p_{\rm part}+V_0a\) for a right-null basis \(V_0\). Given a prior center
\(p_0\), \(Q\succ0\), and a feasible datum \(C\in\operatorname{Ran}A\),
the unique minimizer over real vectors of
\(\frac12(p-p_0)^{\mathsf T}Q(p-p_0)\) subject to \(Ap=C\) is
\[
\boxed{
p_*=p_0+Q^{-1}A^{\mathsf T}
(AQ^{-1}A^{\mathsf T})^+(C-Ap_0).}
\]
If $C\notin\operatorname{Ran}A$, no constrained minimizer exists; the displayed
pseudoinverse expression instead leaves a nonzero constraint residual. Even
when feasible, it need not give a nonnegative spectrum. Positivity therefore
requires a separate check or a declared constrained optimization, whose
solution is not in general the displayed expression.
The run publishes the singular values, rank threshold, right-null basis,
resolution operator, unresolved projector, positivity active set, and prior
sensitivity. The output type is \textsc{ConditionalRadialContinuation}.

\textbf{Theorem (unrestricted tomographic uniqueness).}
\label{thm:oph330-radial-tomography}
For
\[
 C_\ell(r,r')=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty k^2P_\zeta(k)
 j_\ell(kr)j_\ell(kr')\,dk,
\]
the covariance operator on $L^2(r^2dr)$ obeys
\[
 Q_\ell=\mathcal H_\ell^{-1}M_{P_\zeta}\mathcal H_\ell.
\]
Thus a complete radial cross-covariance operator determines $P_\zeta$ uniquely
almost everywhere on its natural operator domain. Fixing one radius $r_0>0$,
the family $C_\ell(r,r_0)$ known for all $r$ suffices, provided that this
section admits spherical-Hankel inversion in the declared function or
distribution class: its spherical-Hankel transform is
$\sqrt{2/\pi}\,P_\zeta(k)j_\ell(kr_0)$, and the Bessel zeros are discrete.
A finite collection of auto-spectra does not supply this tomography theorem.
The positive-radius condition is essential: $j_\ell(kr_0)$ is identically zero
at $r_0=0$ for $\ell\ge1$, so that section contains no spectral information.

\textit{Proof.}
Apply spherical-Hankel inversion in the first radial variable.  Unitary
conjugation exposes the multiplication operator; equal multiplication operators
have equal multipliers almost everywhere.  \hfill$\square$
