\subsection{Readback through destructive seam averaging}
\label{sec:source-seam-path-tomography}

A seam mean destroys an endpoint difference in its mutable state, but this
alone does not obstruct communication when an observer retains local
readback records. Consider a simple path of $d$ seams with arbitrary real
initial port values $(x_0,\ldots,x_d)$. No other operation touches the path
during the following declared schedule. For $k=d-1,d-2,\ldots,0$, apply
canonical pair means successively on seams
$(k,k+1),(k+1,k+2),\ldots,(d-1,d)$.
The destination records its own initial value and its own value after each
sweep, giving $d+1$ local samples. Every intermediate repair and readback is
retained; the record decoder changes no port value.

\begin{theorem}[Destination-local path reconstruction]
\label{thm:source-seam-path-tomography}
The destination samples determine all $d+1$ initial path values uniquely.
They also determine the changed port state after every completed suffix
calibration. The schedule uses exactly $d(d+1)/2$ pair-mean evaluations, of which
$d(d-1)/2$ calibrate the relays and $d$ perform the final source sweep.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a relay state $B=(b_1,\ldots,b_m)$, a forward sweep has endpoint map
\[
 E_B(x)=\Phi_{b_m}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{b_1}(x),
 \qquad \Phi_b(x)=\frac{x+b}{2}.
\]
Its inverse applies $y\mapsto2y-b_m$, then $y\mapsto2y-b_{m-1}$,
and so on. The destination initially knows its singleton suffix. Suppose
it has reconstructed the initial and current states of the calibrated
suffix. The next endpoint sample and that \emph{current} suffix state
therefore recover the next source value by $E_B^{-1}$. Replaying the same
means gives the new current suffix state. Induction reconstructs the
entire initial vector using only destination samples. Summing the sweep
lengths $1+\cdots+d$ gives the operation count.
\end{proof}

This construction also exposes its precision cost. With the calibrated
relay state fixed, changing $x_0$ by $\delta$ changes the final destination
sample by exactly $2^{-d}\delta$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact conditioning of the local readback map]
\label{thm:source-seam-exactconditioning}
For exact seam dynamics and decoding, let $O_d$ map initial port values,
ordered by distance from the destination, to chronological samples
$y_0,\ldots,y_d$. Then
\[
 \kappa_\infty(O_d)=\|O_d^{-1}\|_\infty=g_d,
 \qquad
 g_d=\frac{(1+\sqrt2)^{d+1}+(1-\sqrt2)^{d+1}}2.
\]
Independently bounded additive sample errors of size $\epsilon\geq0$ give
sharp worst-case reconstruction error $g_d\epsilon$ in the maximum norm.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $u_r^k$ be the value at distance $r$ after the length-$k$ sweep, and
write $Sy_k=y_{k-1}$. For $k\geq1$, the actual means give
\[
 u_0^k=u_1^k=y_k,\qquad
 u_r^k=2u_{r-1}^k-u_{r-2}^{k-1}\quad(2\leq r\leq k).
\]
For $k\geq1$, the initial value at depth $k$ is
$2u_k^k-u_{k-1}^{k-1}$; at depth zero it is $y_0$.
Define $H_0=H_1=1$ and $H_r(z)=2H_{r-1}(z)-zH_{r-2}(z)$.
Induction gives $u_r^k=H_r(S)y_k$ and the recovered initial value
$H_{k+1}(S)y_k$. Thus inverse-row delay polynomials $F_k=H_{k+1}$ obey
$F_0=1$, $F_1=2-z$ and $F_k=2F_{k-1}-zF_{k-2}$.
Their coefficients alternate in sign; their magnitudes combine by addition.
Consequently their row norms satisfy
$g_0=1$, $g_1=3$, $g_k=2g_{k-1}+g_{k-2}$, giving the displayed formula.
Every observation is a convex combination of initial values, so
$\|O_d\|_\infty=1$; the largest inverse-row norm is $g_d$.
Sign-matched sample errors attain the bound.
\end{proof}
At fixed range and reconstruction tolerance, the additional sample precision
scales as $\log_2 g_d=1.27155\ldots\,d+O(1)$ bits, linearly in path length.
This supplies no physical precision or clock guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Bounded gate and retained-sample errors]
\label{cor:source-seam-gateerror}
Use the same fixed schedule.
Let each complete pair-mean map have additive maximum-norm error at most
$\eta$, let each retained sample (including memory drift) have error at most
$\sigma$, and let decoding add error at most $\delta$.
Starting from identical initial port values, the reconstructed vector obeys
\[
 \|\widehat x-x\|_\infty
 \leq g_d\left(\frac{d(d+1)}2\eta+\sigma\right)+\delta.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every exact mean is nonexpansive in the maximum norm, so state error
accumulates by at most $\eta$ per mean. Apply the inverse norm to the retained
samples.
\end{proof}
This sufficient bound assumes the stated noise limits; it is not a
sharp gate-noise bound or a measurement of physical hardware errors.

Independent matrix inversion on embedded W12 paths of lengths $1,4,12,24$
gives respectively $g_d=3,41,47321,1855077841$.
The length-24 baseline uses 300 pair means, 25 protected samples and 351
total initialization, repair, local-readback and decoding events; every
retained mean is active. Equal-endpoint evaluations in the general theorem
are identity updates and assert no strict progress. Its
largest written scalar register payload uses 111 sign, numerator and
denominator bits under the declared convention; event metadata and temporary
arithmetic storage are excluded. Source and nonsource
interventions are independently replayed, together with wrong-law,
missing-calibration and remote-read controls.

The module \texttt{SourceSeamPathTomography} formalizes the exact recursive
inverse, injectivity, seam count and attenuation. Independent verification of
the finite observation matrices and complete versioned histories is supplied
in \texttt{code/source\_routing/}. Initial values, wiring, schedule and protected
readback memory are supplied. This is destructive finite readout transport;
it neither selects the metric-neighbour read law nor supplies repeated
immutable-version reads, an intervention-preserving refinement of the full
layered order, bounded physical precision or a physical clock.
