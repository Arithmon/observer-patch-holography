\subsection{Elementary corollaries of the record and screen structure}
\label{sec:elementary-corollaries}

The finite record algebra, the celestial sphere and the information
projection carry several textbook laws as direct corollaries. Each is stated
below with the exact identity it rests on and with the identification it does
not supply. Exact rational controls for every statement, pinned to this
section, are part of the repository receipts \cite{ophrepo}.

\paragraph{Repeated reads and the Zeno limit.}

\begin{proposition}[Repeatability and the Zeno limit]
\label{prop:zeno-limit}
Let \(\rho\) be a state on the finite accessible algebra of one completed
slice, \(P\) an event projector with \(w=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P)>0\), and
\(\rho_P=P\rho P/w\) the declared L\"uders update. Then
\(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_P P)=1\), and the update is idempotent. Let \(H\) be
a Hermitian generator on the same space, \(U_s=e^{-isH}\), and let the
observer reread \(P\) after each of \(n\) equal steps of total parameter
\(t\). For \(\rho\) supported in the range of \(P\), the probability
\(p_n\) that all \(n\) rereads return \(P\) satisfies
\[
 1-p_n\le \frac{2t^2\|H\|^2}{n},
\]
so \(p_n\to1\) as \(n\to\infty\), and the conditioned state converges to
\(e^{-itPHP}\rho\,e^{itPHP}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Repeatability and idempotence are the L\"uders theorems of the finite
event-algebra library \cite{ophconsensus,ophrepo}. Put \(s=t/n\),
\(A=PU_sP\) and \(B=Pe^{-isPHP}P\); both are contractions on the range of
\(P\). For Hermitian \(X\) the exponential series gives
\(\|e^{-isX}-(1-isX)\|\le s^2\|X\|^2/2\), hence
\(\|A-B\|\le s^2\|H\|^2/2+s^2\|PHP\|^2/2\le s^2\|H\|^2\). Telescoping,
\(\|A^n-B^n\|\le n\|A-B\|\le t^2\|H\|^2/n\). The joint probability of
\(n\) returns is \(p_n=\operatorname{Tr}(A^n\rho A^{n\dagger})\), while
\(B^n=Pe^{-itPHP}P\) is unitary on the range of \(P\), so
\(\operatorname{Tr}(B^n\rho B^{n\dagger})=1\). For contractions
\(|\operatorname{Tr}(X\rho X^\dagger)-\operatorname{Tr}(Y\rho Y^\dagger)|
\le2\|X-Y\|\), which gives the bound; the convergence of the conditioned
state follows from the same estimate.
\end{proof}

A rational control replaces the exponential by the rotation with
\(\tan(\theta_n/2)=1/n\), so \(\cos\theta_n=(n^2-1)/(n^2+1)\), read through
a rank-one projector after every step. The survival with reads is
\(\cos^{2n}\theta_n\), which obeys \(1-\cos^{2n}\theta_n\le4/n\), while the
survival without reads is \(\cos^2(n\theta_n)\) and stays away from one.
The step parameter is a flow parameter and \(H\) is supplied; neither is a
physical clock or a source-selected generator.

\paragraph{Distinguishable records through a port.}

\begin{proposition}[Port capacity]
\label{prop:port-capacity}
Let the interface algebra of port \(p\) act on a Hilbert space of dimension
\(d_p\). A family of states that one read through the port distinguishes
without error has at most \(d_p\) members. One read therefore carries at most
\(\log_2 d_p\) bits, and one read of all twelve ports of a carrier at most
\(\sum_p\log_2 d_p\) bits.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
States distinguished without error by one measurement have pairwise
orthogonal supports, and pairwise orthogonal nonzero vectors are linearly
independent, so there are at most \(d_p\) of them. The port bound adds over
the ports because the boundary space is their tensor product.
\end{proof}

Holevo's bound, an imported result, gives the same ceiling for reads with
error. Neither statement assigns an energy to a record: the energy of one
distinction enters only through the clock and energy calibration, and the
bound itself is a count.

\paragraph{Celestial kinematics.}

\begin{proposition}[Aberration, the null scale factor and rapidity addition]
\label{prop:celestial-kinematics}
Let the celestial sphere carry the action of \(SL(2,\mathbb C)\) by M\"obius
maps of the spacetime and Einstein derivation \cite{ophEinstein}, with stereographic coordinate \(\zeta\), and let each null
direction carry the null vector \(q=(1,\Omega)\). Realize the Lorentz
group on Hermitian \(2\times2\) matrices by \(X\mapsto AXA^\dagger\), under
which \(q\) is the rank-one matrix \(\kappa\kappa^\dagger\) with
\(\kappa=(1,\zeta)\). For \(A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in SL(2,\mathbb C)\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item the direction transforms as \(\zeta\mapsto(c+d\zeta)/(a+b\zeta)\);
\item the time component of \(q\) is multiplied by
 \(\lambda_A(\zeta)=(|a+b\zeta|^2+|c+d\zeta|^2)/(1+|\zeta|^2)\), and
 \(\lambda_A\equiv1\) for every \(A\in SU(2)\);
\item for \(A_k=\operatorname{diag}(k^{-1/2},k^{1/2})\) with \(k>0\), put
 \(v=(k-1/k)/(k+1/k)\), \(\gamma=(k+1/k)/2\) and
 \(\cos\theta=(1-|\zeta|^2)/(1+|\zeta|^2)\). Then
 \(\lambda_{A_k}=\gamma(1-v\cos\theta)\) and the transformed direction obeys
 \(\cos\theta'=(\cos\theta-v)/(1-v\cos\theta)\);
\item \(A_{k_1}A_{k_2}=A_{k_1k_2}\), so \(v(k_1k_2)=(v_1+v_2)/(1+v_1v_2)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With \(X=\kappa\kappa^\dagger\) the time component is
\((|\kappa_0|^2+|\kappa_1|^2)/2\) and the axial component is
\((|\kappa_0|^2-|\kappa_1|^2)/2\), which gives \(\cos\theta\). Under
\(A\), \(\kappa\mapsto A\kappa=(a+b\zeta,\,c+d\zeta)\), which is (1) and
(2); \(SU(2)\) preserves \(|\kappa|\). For \(A_k\), \(A\kappa=(k^{-1/2},
k^{1/2}\zeta)\), so \(\lambda=(1/k+k|\zeta|^2)/(1+|\zeta|^2)\) and
\(\zeta'=k\zeta\); substituting \(|\zeta|^2=(1-\cos\theta)/(1+\cos\theta)\)
gives (3). Diagonal matrices multiply entrywise, which is (4).
\end{proof}

Read with \(\theta\) the angle between the propagation direction of a null
ray and the axis, and \(v\) the axial velocity of a second frame, (3) is
the aberration law and \(\lambda\) is the Doppler factor of that frame; (4)
is the relativistic addition of collinear velocities. The scale factor is a
statement about the null vector on the declared sphere. It becomes a
frequency ratio only through the clock calibration and the physical
spacetime attachment, which this section does not supply.

\paragraph{The canonical distribution as the information projection.}

\begin{proposition}[Gibbs law from one linear constraint]
\label{prop:gibbs-projection}
Let \(\tau\) be a faithful reference law on a finite record set, \(E\) a
real function on it, and \(g_\beta=\tau e^{-\beta E}/Z_\beta\). For every
law \(p\) with \(\langle E\rangle_p=\langle E\rangle_{g_\beta}\),
\[
 D(p\Vert\tau)=D(p\Vert g_\beta)+D(g_\beta\Vert\tau).
\]
Hence \(g_\beta\) is the unique minimizer of \(D(\cdot\Vert\tau)\) among
laws with that expected value: the information projection of the third
axiom under one linear constraint is the canonical distribution, and
\(\beta\) is the multiplier of the constraint.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\(\log(g_\beta/\tau)=-\beta E-\log Z_\beta\) is affine in \(E\), so
\(D(p\Vert\tau)-D(p\Vert g_\beta)=\sum_x p(x)\log(g_\beta/\tau)(x)
=-\beta\langle E\rangle_p-\log Z_\beta\), which by the constraint equals
the same expression under \(g_\beta\), namely \(D(g_\beta\Vert\tau)\).
Uniqueness follows from \(D(p\Vert g_\beta)=0\) only at \(p=g_\beta\).
\end{proof}

The history-level form of this identity and the exponential form of the
projected path law are machine-checked in the information-projection
library \cite{ophrepo}. The energy ledger \(E\) is declared, and \(\beta\)
is free against the modular Hamiltonian alone; a temperature reading
consumes the calibration row.

\paragraph{Occupation laws under a declared exchange sector.}

\begin{proposition}[Fermi--Dirac, Bose--Einstein and truncated occupation]
\label{prop:occupation-laws}
Consider one mode of energy \(\varepsilon\) at chemical potential \(\mu\)
and inverse temperature \(\beta\), and put \(x=e^{-\beta(\varepsilon-\mu)}\).
The Gibbs law of Proposition~\ref{prop:gibbs-projection} on the occupation
ledger, with weight \(x^n\) for occupation \(n\), has mean occupation
\(x/(1+x)=1/(e^{\beta(\varepsilon-\mu)}+1)\) when the mode admits the
occupations \(\{0,1\}\), \(x/(1-x)=1/(e^{\beta(\varepsilon-\mu)}-1)\) when
it admits every \(n\ge0\) and \(x<1\), and
\(\sum_{n\le m}nx^n/\sum_{n\le m}x^n\) when it is cut at \(m\); the last
equals the first at \(m=1\) and increases to the second as \(m\to\infty\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Geometric sums: \(\sum_{n\ge0}x^n=1/(1-x)\) and
\(\sum_{n\ge0}nx^n=x/(1-x)^2\); the two-term case and the truncated case
are the same sums cut off. Monotonicity in \(m\) holds because each added
term has occupation above the previous mean.
\end{proof}

The exchange sector is a declared premise of this proposition. Nothing in
it selects the sector, and the two laws stand as the two admissible sectors
of one information projection.

\paragraph{Coarse-graining of the record law.}

\begin{proposition}[Record identification does not raise the entropy of the record law]
\label{prop:record-coarse-graining}
Let \(r\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma'\) be a map on public records that identifies
some of them, and let \(p\) be a law on \(\Sigma\) with push-forward
\(r_*p\). Then \(H(r_*p)\le H(p)\) for the Shannon entropy and
\(\sum_y(r_*p)(y)^2\ge\sum_x p(x)^2\), with equality in both exactly when
\(r\) is injective on the support of \(p\). If \(K\) is a stochastic
repair kernel with stationary law \(\pi\), then
\(D_f(pK\Vert\pi)\le D_f(p\Vert\pi)\) for every \(f\)-divergence, in
particular for the relative entropy and the \(\chi^2\) divergence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\(H(r_*p)=H(p)-H(p\mid r_*p)\) and the conditional entropy is nonnegative,
vanishing exactly when \(r\) is injective on the support. For the collision
sum, \((a+b)^2\ge a^2+b^2\) for \(a,b\ge0\), with equality only when one
vanishes. The kernel statement is the data-processing inequality for
\(f\)-divergences applied to the pair \((p,\pi)\) with \(\pi K=\pi\).
\end{proof}

This is a statement about the law of the public records under repair. It
does not identify that entropy with a thermodynamic entropy; the four-law
package makes that identification under its declared repair law and
calibration.
