\subsection{Conditional scale transfer in the large settled configurations}
\label{subsec:native-terminal-scale-transfer}
The retained level-eight through level-ten configurations comprise sixteen
repair schedules per level, conditional on one initial load field at each
level. For equal-count ancestral groups, let \(x\) and \(y_s\) be the
initial and terminal group means, centered with the same group weights.
The response is \(b_s=\langle x,y_s\rangle/\langle x,x\rangle\).
For \(S\) schedules define
\[
 P_{\rm tot}=\frac1S\sum_s\|y_s\|^2,\quad
 P_{\rm noise}=\frac1{S-1}\sum_s\|y_s-\bar y\|^2,\quad
 P_{\rm common}=\frac1{S(S-1)}\sum_{s\ne t}\langle y_s,y_t\rangle.
\]
Then \(P_{\rm tot}=P_{\rm noise}+P_{\rm common}\) exactly.
Conditional independent schedule sampling makes these unbiased estimates
of schedule variance and squared conditional mean. The finite common-power
estimate may be negative and is not clipped. For disjoint groups with
\(m_g\) ports and normalized weights \(a_g\), the uniform balanced
fixed-total null gives the expected centered group variance
\(\kappa\sum_g(a_g-a_g^2)/m_g\).

\paragraph{Binary port-pattern census.}
The sixty proper rotations of the twelve-port carrier act on its binary
occupancy patterns. Burnside's lemma gives the orbit polynomial
\[
 \frac{(1+x)^{12}+15(1+x^2)^6+20(1+x^3)^4
       +24(1+x)^2(1+x^5)^2}{60}.
\]
The coefficients for zero through twelve raised ports are
$1,1,3,5,12,14,24,14,12,5,3,1,1$, totaling 96 rotation orbits.
Each of four retained schedules at each of levels six, eight, nine and ten
contains all 96 classes. The census receipts and an independent polynomial
recalculation accompany the same verifier. This counts local binary
readout classes, without identifying them as particle species or replacing
the spatial configuration by its orbit counts.

\subsection{Port transport, internal response and metric sensitivity}
\label{subsec:native-transport-evidence}
The same seam law has mean propagator \(e^{-tL/2}\). For \(N\)
twelve-port carriers, resolved low eigenpairs give \(N\lambda_2\) equal
to \(0.882851,0.825266,0.817473,0.816553,0.815798\) at levels zero
through four. Separate level-five through level-seven solves give
\(0.815612814,0.815530307,0.815499887\); their largest relative
eigenpair residuals are \(2.92\times10^{-10},2.24\times10^{-9},
1.18\times10^{-9}\). These are finite floating-point solves, not
interval bounds on the gap or a proof of a refinement limit.

In orthonormal carrier-mean and internal-port coordinates, write
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\ B^{\mathsf T}&C\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Internal connectivity makes \(C\) positive definite. Completing the
square proves the exact static identity
\[
 \min_v\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf T}
 L\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}
 =u^{\mathsf T}(A-BC^{-1}B^{\mathsf T})u,
 \qquad v=-C^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}u.
\]
At level zero this Schur complement reduces dipole-rate error from
\(42.9\)--\(44.2\%\) to \(0.348\)--\(0.363\%\), with internal
stationarity residual \(1.20\times10^{-15}\). It is a zero-frequency
reduction; exact dynamic elimination replaces \(C^{-1}\) by the
frequency-dependent resolvent. At level four the first mode has only
\(1.46\times10^{-5}\) of its squared norm in internal variation,
yet the naive coordinate-trial rate is about \(50\%\) too large.

Band averages do not establish rotational invariance. At level four the
relative spread of the seven-mode band is \(14.642\%\) for the actual
port graph, \(13.115\%\) for unweighted cell adjacency and \(0.01561\%\)
for a separately supplied round-metric cotangent operator. This exploratory
metric control is a different operator. Inverting a precision does not
remove its anisotropy: on the mean-zero space,
\([L^{-1},U]=-L^{-1}[L,U]L^{-1}\). A heat-trace calculation with bounded
omitted modes admits a level-two window of screen spectral dimension
\(1.938\)--\(2.016\); the fixed higher-level eigenpair budgets do not
resolve the prescribed heat-trace window. This is a boundary-screen
diffusion diagnostic, not a measurement of bulk spacetime dimension.
The source-pinned solves and metric controls reside under
\path{sim-analysis/codex/dynamics/} in the same evidence package.

\subsection{Absolute refinement covariance and nested records}
\label{subsec:native-refinement-covariance}
The retained refinement sample contains 61 chains: 18 level-seven to
level-eight chains, 32 level-eight to level-nine chains, and 11 shuffled
controls. Four child carriers inherit their parent's twelve-port record;
independent fresh integer loads are drawn uniformly from \(0,\ldots,4\).
All 61 retained chains settle. Eight ancestral group sizes give 488
covariance rows. Let \(x,f,y\) denote centered inherited, fresh and
settled group means. Seeded reconstruction of \(\operatorname{Var}(f)\),
the archived variance ratio and the simple and joint normal equations
recover their absolute covariance matrix. All rows satisfy the retained
positive-semidefinite and regression checks; 256 independently retained
parent variances agree to maximum relative error \(4.15\times10^{-13}\).
Fresh arrays were not independently hashed at production, so that part of
the reconstruction remains conditional on the pinned producer and random
number generator semantics, including the shuffled-control permutation.

For unshuffled level-eight to level-nine chains, the mean inherited-only
residual variance falls from \(0.0108181853\) at four carriers per group
to \(2.55666955\times10^{-6}\) at 65,536. At the latter scale, removing
the fitted fresh-load response too leaves \(4.00020779\times10^{-8}\).
Multiplying the inherited-only variance by group size gives \(0.16755\),
close to the supplied fresh port-average variance \(2/12=1/6\).
The joint inherited response at group sizes
\(256,1024,4096,16384,65536\) is respectively
\(0.905427,0.956213,0.978740,0.991953,0.994902\).
The corresponding \(-\log_2 b^2\) values vary from \(0.286815\) to
\(0.014788\); they are not one common spectral tilt.

For equal nested bins, lift all residuals to the same uniformly selected
finest bin. If \(e_M\) is the least-squares residual after averaging to
groups of size \(M\), then
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(e_M,e_N)=\operatorname{Var}(e_{\max(M,N)}).
\]
Indeed, averaging a fine residual into a coarse bin differs from the
coarse residual by a linear combination of the coarse predictors. The
coarse normal equations make this difference orthogonal to the coarse
residual. Thus successive scale differences are orthogonal and their
variances are differences of the residual variances. Orthogonality is
not independence. This identity concerns the same nested location; it
does not determine covariance at separated sky positions. Parent schedules
share initial data, and linear spatial residuals are not innovations
conditioned on a complete observer history. The original chain receipts,
absolute extractor and nested transformation are preserved under
\path{sim-analysis/codex/source_derivation/innovations/} and
\path{sim-analysis/data/refine_ensemble/} in the native-repair package.
