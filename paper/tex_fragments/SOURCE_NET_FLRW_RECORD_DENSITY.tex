\subsection{Expansion as record density on the source order}
\label{sec:flrw-record-density}

The spatially flat Friedmann--Lema\^{\i}tre--Robertson--Walker metric in
conformal time $\eta$ is
\begin{equation}
 ds^2=\sigma(\eta)^2\bigl(c^2\,d\eta^2-|dx|^2\bigr),
 \label{flrw:eq-metric}
\end{equation}
with scale factor $\sigma(\eta)>0$; the letter $a$ is the edge radius of the
read law. This metric is a positive conformal factor times the flat metric
in the coordinates $(\eta,x)$. A positive conformal factor leaves the causal
order of a Lorentzian metric unchanged, and the causal order fixes the
metric up to such a factor (Hawking, King and McCarthy 1976; Malament 1977).
On the spatially flat family the conformal factor is the entire background,
so every profile $\sigma$ has the causal order of flat space in comoving
coordinates. The layered read law of the source-net construction places an
event at every $(j,s)$ for a site $s$ in a population $S$, lets $(j+1,t)$
read $(j,s)$ whenever $\|t-s\|\le a$, and takes the reflexive transitive
closure of the reads as the order. Written in comoving coordinates with the
tick $\Delta=a/c$ in conformal time, this law generates one finite order for
every profile. The profile enters only through the count measure.

\begin{proposition}[Conformal invariance of the layered order]
\label{flrw:order-invariance}
Let $S$ be a subset of a real normed space, $a>0$ an edge radius and
$\sigma_j>0$ the scale at layer $j$. The physical position of the site $s$
at layer $j$ is $\sigma_js$ and the physical read radius at that layer is
$\sigma_ja$. The physical read from $(j,s)$ to $(j+1,t)$,
\[
 \|\sigma_{j+1}t-\sigma_{j+1}s\|\le\sigma_{j+1}a,
\]
holds exactly when $\|t-s\|\le a$. The layered orders generated by the
physical reads and by the comoving reads coincide, for every profile
$(\sigma_j)_{j\ge0}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\sigma>0$, $\|\sigma t-\sigma s\|=\sigma\|t-s\|$, and multiplication
by $\sigma$ preserves and reflects the comparison with $\sigma a$. The two
read relations are equal, hence so are their reflexive transitive closures.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[FLRW count measure]
\label{flrw:count-measure}
Assign a comoving cell of volume $v_s$ to every site $s$ and the conformal
tick $\Delta$ to every layer. Under a constant physical event density
$\rho$, the physical tick at layer $j$ is $\sigma_j\Delta$ and the physical
cell volume is $\sigma_j^3v_s$, so a finite set $I$ of events carries the
mass
\begin{equation}
 M_\sigma(I)=\rho\sum_{(j,s)\in I}(\sigma_j\Delta)(\sigma_j^3v_s)
 =\rho\sum_{(j,s)\in I}\sigma_j^4\,\Delta\,v_s,
 \label{flrw:eq-mass}
\end{equation}
the discrete form of the four-volume weight $\sigma^4\,d\eta\,d^3x$. Write
$M_1(I)=\rho\Delta\sum_{(j,s)\in I}v_s$ for the flat mass. If
$0\le\sigma_{\min}\le\sigma_j\le\sigma_{\max}$ on the layers of $I$, then
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{\min}^4M_1(I)\le M_\sigma(I)\le\sigma_{\max}^4M_1(I).
 \label{flrw:eq-sandwich}
\end{equation}
A constant profile $\sigma_j=\sigma$ gives $M_\sigma(I)=\sigma^4M_1(I)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The identity in \eqref{flrw:eq-mass} is the product of the two scaled
factors. Fourth powers are monotone on nonnegative reals and
$\Delta v_s\ge0$, so each term of the sum lies between the same term with
$\sigma_j$ replaced by $\sigma_{\min}$ and by $\sigma_{\max}$. Summing
proves \eqref{flrw:eq-sandwich}; the constant case is the common factor
$\sigma^4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Count clock in an expanding family]
\label{flrw:count-clock}
Let $I$ and $J$ be finite event sets with uniform cells, $J$ nonempty,
with $\sigma^I_{\min}\le\sigma_j\le\sigma^I_{\max}$ on the layers of $I$,
$\sigma^J_{\min}\le\sigma_j\le\sigma^J_{\max}$ on the layers of $J$,
$\sigma^I_{\min}\ge0$ and $\sigma^J_{\min}>0$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\sigma^I_{\min}}{\sigma^J_{\max}}
 \left(\frac{|I|}{|J|}\right)^{1/4}
 \le\left(\frac{M_\sigma(I)}{M_\sigma(J)}\right)^{1/4}
 \le\frac{\sigma^I_{\max}}{\sigma^J_{\min}}
 \left(\frac{|I|}{|J|}\right)^{1/4}.
 \label{flrw:eq-clock-enclosure}
\end{equation}
Let $I$ and $J$ approximate comoving diamonds of conformal durations $T_I$
and $T_J$ in the sense of the count-clock theorem for the flat order, so
that $(|I|/|J|)^{1/4}\to T_I/T_J$ under refinement. A comoving worldline
through a diamond of conformal duration $T$ accrues proper time between
$\sigma_{\min}T$ and $\sigma_{\max}T$. In the refinement limit the clock
read from the physical masses, $(M_\sigma(I)/M_\sigma(J))^{1/4}$, and the
proper-time ratio $\tau_I/\tau_J$ lie in one interval whose endpoints have
the quotient
$(\sigma^I_{\max}/\sigma^I_{\min})(\sigma^J_{\max}/\sigma^J_{\min})$; the
two readings agree up to this factor in either direction. For a profile
continuous in $\eta$ and diamonds shrinking to points, the factor tends to
one and the clock reads the proper-time ratio exactly in the limit.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Divide the two sandwich inequalities \eqref{flrw:eq-sandwich} for $I$ and
$J$, with $M_1(I)/M_1(J)=|I|/|J|$ for uniform cells, and take the
increasing fourth root; the fourth root of $\sigma^4$ is $\sigma$. The
proper time along the comoving worldline is $\int\sigma\,d\eta$ over the
conformal duration, bounded by the extreme scales times $T$; the ratio of
two such enclosures has the stated endpoint quotient. Continuity of
$\sigma$ forces $\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}\to1$ on shrinking diamonds.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Redshift as a count ratio]
\label{flrw:redshift}
Let two congruent comoving diamonds, with the same comoving cells and the
same conformal ticks, lie at the emission epoch, of scale $\sigma_e$, and
at the reception epoch, of scale $\sigma_0$, the profile being constant
across each. Their masses are $n_e=\kappa\sigma_e^4$ and
$n_0=\kappa\sigma_0^4$ with the common factor
$\kappa=\rho\Delta\sum_{(j,s)\in I}v_s$, and
\begin{equation}
 1+z=\frac{\sigma_0}{\sigma_e}=\left(\frac{n_0}{n_e}\right)^{1/4}.
 \label{flrw:eq-redshift}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The constant case of Proposition~\ref{flrw:count-measure} gives both
masses; $\kappa$ cancels in the ratio, and the positive fourth root of
$(\sigma_0/\sigma_e)^4$ is $\sigma_0/\sigma_e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Expansion history as a record-production law]
\label{flrw:record-production}
On this family the Friedmann equation is a statement about record
production: a comoving cell of volume $v$ produces
$\rho\,\sigma(\eta)^4\,\Delta\,v$ records per conformal tick, and a profile
$\sigma(\eta)$ is the same datum as a record-production rate per comoving
cell. The de Sitter profile $\sigma(\eta)=-1/(H\eta)$ on $\eta<0$ solves
$d\sigma/d\eta=H\sigma^2$, and its record density per comoving cell is
$n(\eta)\propto(H\eta)^{-4}$; the fixed-capacity branch of the corpus,
$w=-1$, corresponds to this profile. The Lean module
\texttt{Lean/Geometry/SourceNetConformalRecordDensity.lean} checks the
order invariance, the mass identity and sandwich, the clock enclosure
\eqref{flrw:eq-clock-enclosure}, the redshift identity and the de Sitter
derivative and density identities over the reals.

Three items lie outside this construction. The source law selecting the
profile, the record-production rate per comoving cell, is not derived; the
profile is a supplied datum. A reweighted flat order determines at most the
conformal factor; on the spatially flat family that factor is the entire
background, and spatial curvature $k\neq0$ changes the comoving spatial
metric, which this construction does not treat. No physical clock and no
population selection by native repair follow from these identities.
\end{remark}
