\begin{abstract}
Observer Patch Holography (OPH) seeks an executable realization of its
observer algebra whose compatible inter-observer measurements reproduce the
observed Universe, with quantum-field, classical and gravitational physics
entering as controlled effective descriptions of those measurements. This
paper states the executable construction as a technical contract. The
specification consists of a twelve-port carrier with icosahedral incidence,
a native local repair law that writes a coin and current record at every
event, a finite observer algebra of preparations, measurements, retained
records and record-only feedback, declared services, and two accepted closure
inputs. We give acceptance criteria for the completion target and report the
certified components. A two-event observer whose retained record selects its
next repair moves a final occupation from $1/6$ to $35/264$. Under uniform seam attempts the repair channels with discarded records have the exact quantum spectral gap $(5-\sqrt5)/60$, and complete classical records recover every initial load population and permit an optimal recovery of the unknown quantum input with explicitly computed fidelity below one. A binary detector converts the electromagnetic coupling computed from a closure input into a recorded bit that controls the next repair, and scalar-field detectors carry refinement, occupation and complete-output error bounds. In a common source, quantum
electric flux, charged fermions and a scalar field share one register; for a
declared preparation its recorded feedback response is certified nonzero at
infinite oscillator occupation. A fixed count protocol distinguishes two quantum record laws with
identical classical transitions; no comparison data exist. A premise map
records which theorem antecedents the construction supplies and which enter
as inputs. The open connections are spatial refinement, responsive geometry,
physical clock calibration and independent empirical comparison.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Observer Patch Holography (OPH) treats observations as primary. The
completion target of its construction programme is an executable realization
of the OPH observer algebra whose compatible inter-observer measurements
reproduce the observed Universe. Reproduction is judged on
observations. The joint statistics of those measurements, together with their
responses to interventions, must admit the observed 3+1-dimensional
quantum-field, classical and gravitational descriptions as controlled
effective descriptions, each in its regime of validity. No fundamental field
or spacetime is reconstructed first. Fields, particles and geometry enter as
effective descriptions of what observers measure and jointly interpret, and
the data structures of an implementation do not change that ontology
\cite{mCONSTRUCTION,mINTERP}.

OPH starts from one hypothesis, stated in full in the basis document and the flagship synthesis \cite{basis,flagship}: the Universe is a timeless, self-referential structure whose observers recover the specification of their own world from their records and realize it, so that the structure reads itself into existence. Because nothing lies outside it, whatever observers recover and implement needs no further justification, nothing may be assumed that observers have not recovered, and agreement fixes what counts as shared; the carrier, the observer algebra, the repair law and the closure inputs $P$ and $N$ are justified by being recovered, by running, and by reproducing what observers measure. The executable specification is where the account enters the loop; the loop itself has no first step. Here $P$ is the screen grain, the pixel ratio of one screen cell, and $N$ a record capacity in nats; Section~\ref{sec:closure-inputs} states how the construction uses them.

The order of exposition enters at the specification, because it is concrete
and because observers stand there, holding records and inferring. The order
of justification puts the hypothesis first, since the hypothesis is what
exempts the specification from external justification. This paper stays at
the entry point. It treats the executable specification as a candidate and
writes down the contract by which that candidate is judged. The contract
states what is specified and what counts as completion, which components are
certified and with which exact results, which theorem antecedents those
components supply, and the open connections to the completion target.

Two companion bodies of work frame that contract. The flagship synthesis
\cite{flagship} gives the informal account of OPH and selected results. The
theorem library holds conditional theorems on consensus and repair
\cite{ophconsensus}, observers and records \cite{ophobservers}, gauge
structure \cite{ophgauge}, spacetime and Einstein dynamics
\cite{ophspacetime}, and screen microphysics \cite{ophmicro}. The labels A1,
A2 and A3 used there name theorem premises. A1 is the carrier, federation and
response layer of the executable specification, A2 the agreement condition
with its endogeneity clause, and A3 the least-informative state selection
that the no-outside rule imposes. A theorem that uses them states an
implication. Section~\ref{sec:premises} records, route by route, which of
those antecedents the executable construction supplies on its own finite
objects.

Section~\ref{sec:spec} states the specification, namely the carrier, the
native repair law and its contrast-record variant, the finite observer
algebra, the declared services, the closure inputs and the time parameters.
Section~\ref{sec:target} states the completion target as five obligations

observer and its records, scalar field detectors, a common interacting source in which

Section~\ref{sec:premises} gives the premise map together with four retained

\section{The specification}
\label{sec:spec}

\subsection{The carrier and its load configurations}
\label{sec:carrier}

The specification has one carrier with twelve primitive ports. Its incidence
is the oriented boundary of an icosahedron, with thirty seams and twenty
faces; five seams meet at every port, the neighbours of each port form a
five-cycle, and every seam lies on two oppositely oriented faces
\cite{ophmicro,flagship}. Write $A$ for the adjacency matrix of the seam
graph and $L=5I-A$ for its Laplacian. The spectrum of $L$ is $0$,
$5-\sqrt5$ with multiplicity three, $6$ with multiplicity five and
$5+\sqrt5$ with multiplicity three, so its characteristic polynomial is
$x(x-6)^5(x^2-10x+20)^3$ \cite{oQM,rQM,csSLOW}.

A load configuration assigns a nonnegative integer $x_p$ to each port $p$,
with total load $\sum_p x_p=2$. There are 78 configurations. The 66 with two
distinct occupied ports form the binary shell; the other twelve are
collisions $\ket{2u}$ with load two at one port $u$. We write $\Hil_{78}=\C^{78}$
for the quantum load space with basis $\ket{x}$, $\Hil_{66}$ for the span of
the binary shell, and $M_{78}$, $M_{66}$ for the corresponding full matrix
algebras. For a binary configuration occupied at ports $u$ and $w$ we also write $\ket{uw}$ or $\ket{\{u,w\}}$.

Ports are numbered $0$ to $11$ by the frame vectors $u_p$: $(-1,\varphi,0)$, $(1,\varphi,0)$, $(-1,-\varphi,0)$, $(1,-\varphi,0)$, $(0,-1,\varphi)$, $(0,1,\varphi)$, $(0,-1,-\varphi)$, $(0,1,-\varphi)$, $(\varphi,0,-1)$, $(\varphi,0,1)$, $(-\varphi,0,-1)$, $(-\varphi,0,1)$, with $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. The thirty seams are the pairs with $\lvert u_a-u_b\rvert^2=4$, each written $(a,b)$ with $a<b$, and that order is the orientation used by the repair law below \cite{csSLOW,csCAUS}.

The theorem library labels A1 the layer formed by carriers, their federation
through typed seams and coherent triple overlaps, the oriented spherical support, and the complete reversible port response \cite{axref}. The executed construction realizes one carrier of that layer, namely its ports, seams and faces, its
load configurations and the repair events on its seams. Coherent quantum
operations run through twelve-port source controls. A six-level processor
carries twelve source generators, one for each port, and every executed
gate is the exponential of a real combination of these generators, applied
to two- and four-dimensional proper codes through declared load and export
services \cite{csDERIV,ssREADME}. Federation across carriers, the spherical
support, and a physical current for the complete response algebra are not
executed; Section~\ref{sec:premises} lists them among the supplied
antecedents.

\subsection{Native repair and its contrast-record variant}
\label{sec:repair}

Fix an oriented seam $e=(a,b)$ and a fair placement coin $c\in\{0,1\}$. The
native repair law is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:native}
d=x_a-x_b,\qquad
j=\tfrac12\bigl[d-(2c-1)(\lvert d\rvert \bmod 2)\bigr],\qquad
y=x+j\,(\mathbf e_b-\mathbf e_a),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf e_p$ is the unit load vector of port $p$ and $y$ is the
repaired configuration. The two endpoint loads become the floor and the
ceiling of their mean; the coin assigns the ceiling to $a$ when $c=1$; all
other loads are unchanged. The record is $r=(c,j)$, the coin and the signed
current. Compatibility on a seam is specified independently of the update;
it requires the two endpoint loads to differ by at most one. On the quantum load space the
record defines the current instrument $\Cn$ with Kraus operators and
record-discarded channel
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kraus}
L^{e}_{(c,j)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\sum_{x:\,j(x,c)=j}\ket{y(x,c)}\bra{x},
\qquad
\Cn_e(\rho)=\sum_{r}L^{e}_{r}\,\rho\,L^{e\dagger}_{r}.
\end{equation}
Both maps are written in Kraus form \cite{kraus1983}. The contrast-record
variant $\En$ keeps $r=(c,d)$ instead, with Kraus operators $M^{e}_{(c,d)}=2^{-1/2}\sum_{x:\,x_a-x_b=d}\ket{y(x,c)}\bra{x}$ and
record-discarded channel $\En_e$. Uniform independent seam attempts form the
baseline schedule; on diagonal binary states the repair is then the lazy symmetric exclusion process with two particles, with generator $\frac1{60}\sum_e(S_e-I)$ per attempt, $S_e$ exchanging the endpoint loads \cite{mCANDIDATES,oQM}.

\begin{proposition}[Native repair instrument]
\label{prop:native}
Let $e=(a,b)$ be an oriented seam of the twelve-port carrier, let the
placement coin be fair, and let $\Cn_e$ and $\En_e$ act on $\Hil_{78}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item For every record value the branch map $x\mapsto y(x,c)$ is an injective
translation of its support. Consequently $\sum_rL^{e\dagger}_rL^e_r=I$, and
$\Cn_e$ is completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP), also when
tensored with an untouched reference system. The same holds for $\En_e$.
\item Every branch conserves the total load, leaves spectator ports
unchanged, never increases $\sum_px_p^2$, and maps the binary shell into
itself.
\item The current labels satisfy $c\in\{0,1\}$ and $j\in\{-1,0,1\}$; all six
labels occur on $\Hil_{78}$ and four occur on the binary shell.
\item $\Cn$ and $\En$ induce the same classical transition on every
configuration, and
\[
\En_e^2=\En_e,\qquad \En_e\Cn_e=\Cn_e\En_e=\En_e,\qquad
\Cn_e^n=\En_e+2^{1-n}(\Cn_e-\En_e)\quad(n\ge1).
\]
On the binary shell $\En_e$ is a trace-preserving conditional expectation.
\item With the seam-total parity $p=(x_a+x_b)\bmod 2$ one has
$d=2j+(2c-1)p$, and $\En$ equals $\Cn$ followed by a binary record of $p$
and this relabelling, as an identity of complete unnormalized branches.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The inverse of a fixed-current branch adds $j$ at $a$ and subtracts it at
$b$; every basis vector has one branch for each coin, so each column of the
joint record/source isometry has two entries $2^{-1/2}$ with disjoint
supports \cite{oDERIV}. For fixed contrast, the unchanged spectators and the
endpoint sum reconstruct the input, which gives (i) for $\En$. Items (ii) and
(iv) are the single-seam relations of the candidate register
\cite{mCANDIDATES}. Item (v) follows from $j=\lceil d/2\rceil$ for $c=0$ and
$j=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$ for $c=1$; the parity-refined Kraus operator
$\Pi_pL_{(c,j)}$ has exactly the contrast group as support
\cite{sdDERIV}. The executable checks all 312 outputs of the observer's two
seams against an independent floor and ceiling rule \cite{oDERIV}.
\end{proof}

The current instrument is not a classical coarse-graining of the contrast
instrument. Grouping the labels of $\En$ sums density operators and cannot
restore the coherent Kraus sums of $\Cn$. The classical repair rule alone
selects neither instrument, and Section~\ref{sec:prediction} makes the choice
observable. The candidate register of the construction also records laws that
couple the native current to private six-level response factors at the two
seam endpoints \cite{mCANDIDATES}. One of them, a reciprocal coherent
exchange named N4, enters the local-tick certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:local-tick}; the other components use the native law and its
contrast variant.

\subsection{The finite observer algebra}
\label{sec:observer-algebra}

An observer of length $n$ on the carrier consists of a preparation on $\Hil_{78}$, a record tape $R^{\otimes n}$, seam policies $s_k$ that map committed records and classical randomness to seams or to supplied detector outcomes, the coherent writes $W_{s_k}$, optional supplied instruments whose committed bits may enter $s_k$, and a halting rule after $n$ writes; a policy reads only committed records. Definition~\ref{def:observer} is the instance $n=2$, and Sections~\ref{sec:p-detector}, \ref{sec:fields} and \ref{sec:common} add declared instruments to it.

\begin{definition}[Two-event native observer]
\label{def:observer}
Let $R=\C^6$ carry one basis vector per current label $(c,j)$. The observer
carrier is
\[
\Hil_{\rm cap}=\Hil_{78}\oplus(R\otimes\Hil_{78})\oplus(R\otimes R\otimes\Hil_{78}),
\]
with sectors of dimensions 78, 468 and 2808, total 3354; the sector index is
the phase, the number of filled record slots.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item \emph{Preparation.} The scored preparation is uniform on the 66 binary
configurations; the full input family uses all 78.
\item \emph{Measurement and record.} At phase zero the coherent write
$W_{e}=\sum_r\ket{r}\otimes L^{e}_r$ acts on the seam $e=(0,1)$.
\item \emph{Record-only feedback.} At phase one the seam is $s(r)=(0,5)$ if
the stored current is positive and $(0,1)$ otherwise, and
$V_1(\ket r\otimes\psi)=\ket r\otimes W_{s(r)}\psi$.
\item \emph{Forecast.} Before the second event the controller reads its
stored letter and issues a supplied forecast $q(r)$ of a positive second
current.
\item \emph{Halting.} At phase two the observer acts as the identity.
\end{enumerate}
The controller reads only the committed first record; it has no access to
source loads or future outcomes.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[One CPTP map with classical-prefix preservation]
\label{prop:cptp}
Let $V_0=W_{(0,1)}$ and $V_1$ be extended by zero to the other sectors, giving
$A_0$ and $A_1$, and let $P_2$ project onto the full sector. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $V_0$ and $V_1$ are isometries on every nonfull sector, including
unreachable combinations of letters and loads, and
$T(\rho)=A_0\rho A_0^\dagger+A_1\rho A_1^\dagger+P_2\rho P_2$ is CPTP;
\item on the inhabited subspace $K=\Hil_1\oplus\Hil_2$ every first-record
projector $Q_r$ obeys $T^*(Q_r)=Q_r$, so a committed first record is reread
with probability one;
\item for the blank input with occupations $(1,1,0,\dots,0)$, the
first-record projector of the letter $(0,0)$, extended by zero to phase zero,
has expectation $0$ before the tick and $1/2$ after it, so writing a record is
a transition distinct from preserving it;
\item for the equal superposition of $\{0,2\}$ and $\{1,2\}$, the
reduced-density entry of the first-record pair $((0,1),(0,0))$ equals $1/4$
after the first write and $1/8$ after the second while the classical
diagonals are unchanged, so coherence between different record values need
not survive feedback.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Orthogonal retained labels and $W_s^\dagger W_s=I$ give
$V_0^\dagger V_0=V_1^\dagger V_1=I$, hence $A_0^\dagger A_0+A_1^\dagger
A_1+P_2=I$. Every subsequent map intertwines $Q_r$ on $K$. In (iv) the
working state attached to the entry has occupations $\{1,2\}$, on which the
isometries of $(0,5)$ and $(0,1)$ overlap by $1/2$ \cite{oDERIV}. The
checkpoint adapter verifies all $78+468+2808$ columns and both the
preservation identity on the 3276-dimensional checkpoint space and the
blank-sector control \cite{csDERIV,rCS}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Feedback requires classical record preservation]
\label{lem:multiplicative}
Let $\Lambda$ be a CPTP map from $R\otimes W$ to $R\otimes W'$ with
$\Lambda^*(B\otimes I)=B\otimes I$ for every $B$ in the full matrix algebra
of $R$. Then $\Lambda^*(I\otimes O)=I\otimes\Phi^*(O)$ for a unital
completely positive map $\Phi^*$, so changing only the record preparation of
a product input changes no output statistic on $W'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The operators $B\otimes I$ lie in the multiplicative domain of the unital
completely positive map $\Lambda^*$, because the Schwarz inequality is an
equality for them \cite{choi1974}. Then $\Lambda^*(I\otimes O)$ commutes with
every $B\otimes I$ and has the stated form \cite{oDERIV}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplicative} fixes the shape of the observer contract. A
record that steers the working state is preserved as a classical record,
with its projectors fixed, while coherence between different record values
may change.

\subsection{Declared services}
\label{sec:services}

Each component names the services it assumes; they belong to the
specification and enter the resource counts where a component counts
resources. The services are coherent proper-code load and export, with
complementary failure outcomes retained by a complete channel definition;
blank initialization, reset, storage and routing of quantum registers and
classical records; Born readout of pointers and fair coins; and analogue
precision of source pulses. Particular components add a calibrated external
flux and probe (Section~\ref{sec:p-detector}), incident-event recognition
and bare-occupation readout (Section~\ref{sec:local-tick}), and coherent
phase preparation and readout (Section~\ref{sec:prediction}). Inside the
joint instruments of Section~\ref{sec:common}, native integer repair is a
declared primitive whose 78-dimensional channel is not compiled into the six-level processor. The processor algebra is $M_6$; its twelve generators are the exact source generators of the compiler, one per port \cite{ssREADME,csDERIV}; a proper code is a two- or four-dimensional subspace of $\C^6$ with its load and export channels and the complementary failure outcome; and the service error model is the compositional contract of Section~\ref{sec:ce-source}. Exact certificates exclude numerical execution error
and physical service error unless a component states otherwise.

\subsection{The closure inputs \texorpdfstring{$P$}{P} and \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N}}
\label{sec:closure-inputs}

The pixel ratio $P$ is the area of one screen cell in units of
$\ell_\star^2$, and $N$ is a record capacity in nats
\cite{ophobservers,pACCESS}. The construction accepts both as closure-derived
inputs and fits neither to an output. $P$ enters through a declared coupling map, the source-only structured-running branch of the fine-structure closure (one-loop running and matching conventions with the tree-level $m_Z$ closure), defined in the P-derivation code of the repository \cite{ophrepo} and consumed unchanged; the map is a declared selection, not a result of this construction. At the pinned closure value $P=1.630972095858897\ldots$, the midpoint of the pinned forward-closure interval, the map gives $\alpha^{-1}(P)\approx136.9948351774$ and $g(P)=\sqrt{4\pi\alpha(P)}\approx0.302867613117$ in Heaviside--Lorentz natural units, numerical evaluations of the executed map \cite{pREADME,rP}. $N$ enters only as a bound, and no component consumes its closure value. For a declared capacity carrier of dimension $D$, the record capacity $N_{\rm record}=\ln D$ bounds every correctable classical record code by $\ln M_0\le N_{\rm record}$ \cite{mCONSTRUCTION,oCAP}. The
cosmological relation $N=3\pi/(\Lambda\ell_\star^2)$ additionally requires a
physical identification of capacity with horizon area, which the
construction does not make.

\section{The finite observer}

\subsection{The native observer}
\label{sec:native-observer}

The native observer executes Definition~\ref{def:observer} on the scored
preparation \cite{oREADME,oDERIV}. A trial is one fresh preparation followed
by two repair events and a halt; running many trials repeats the preparation
and does not extend one observer's memory. Event order is the time
parameter. The tape, prior, forecast table, seam selector and halting rule
are designed apparatus, and the forecast is a fixed table without learning.

\begin{proposition}[Statistics of the native observer]
\label{prop:native-observer}
Prepare the 66 binary configurations uniformly, use fair coins and the
controller of Definition~\ref{def:observer}, and let $Y$ indicate a positive
second current. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item the 264 histories, two coins for each of the 66 configurations at each
of the two events, each have weight $1/264$;
\item the first record has masses $14/33$, $14/33$, $5/66$, $5/66$ for
$(c,j)=(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,-1)$, the conditional probabilities
$q(r)=\Pr(Y=1\mid r)$ are $0$, $5/56$, $0$, $1/2$ in the same order, and
$\Pr(Y=1)=5/66$;
\item the Brier risk of forecasting $Y$ is $395/7392$ with $q(r)$,
$140/2013$ with the best forecast based on the chosen seam alone, and
$12875/150304$ when $q$ is evaluated on an independent record resampled
within the same seam;
\item the mean final load at port $0$ is $35/264$ under record feedback and
$1/6$ when the selector is disconnected or replaced by an independent
selector with the same seam frequencies; the feedback effect is $-3/88$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For (iii), $\Ex[(Y-q(R))^2]=\Ex[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid R)]$. The chosen
seam $S$ is a function of $R$; the best $S$-only forecast has excess risk
$\Ex[\operatorname{Var}(q(R)\mid S)]$, and an independent resample within the
seam adds twice that amount. For (iv), a positive first current occurs on ten
binary inputs with coin zero, mass $5/66$. On that branch $x_0=0$, $x_1=1$
and $\Ex[x_5]=1/10$; a second repair on $(0,1)$ gives port-0 mean $1/2$ and
one on $(0,5)$ gives $1/20$. Hence the change is
$(5/66)(1/20-1/2)=-3/88$ and the mean is $1/6-3/88=35/264$. An independent
selector leaves the uniform shell uniform under either seam
\cite{oDERIV}.
\end{proof}

The other two current letters occur only on the full carrier, where the
forecast abstains. The exact receipt retains all 264 histories and 18
calculation checks \cite{rNATIVE}. A seeded sequential execution of 20,000
fresh trials (seed 20261012) performs 40,000 native events, matches all
20,000 trials against their exact path references and records 20,000
explicit halts \cite{rRUN}. Its sample means of the final port-0 load are
$0.1327$ with feedback, $0.16645$ with the selector disconnected and
$0.1668$ with the independent selector; the sample Brier risk of the record
forecast is $478659/8960000$, against $395/7392$.

\subsection{Record capacity}
\label{sec:capacity}

The retained records are evaluated with the correctable-public-record
construction of the theorem library \cite{ophobservers,oCAP}. There $M_0$ is
the largest correctable set of reachable public records, the independence
number of the compound checkpoint confusability graph; the record capacity is
$N_{\rm record}=\ln M_0$ nats; and $M_0\le D$ for a declared carrier of
dimension $D$. The counts below describe the reachable record interface;
program, source, probe and service memory are additional.

\begin{proposition}[Record capacity of the native observer]
\label{prop:capacity}
Take halt and exact classical reread as the declared terminal continuations
of the observer of Definition~\ref{def:observer}. Then the compound graph
has no edges, and:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $M_0=11$ on the binary preparation (four reachable first records, 264
histories) and $M_0=17$ on the full family of 78 inputs (six first records,
312 histories);
\item each terminal word projector has rank 78 in the 3354-dimensional
carrier, distinct projectors are orthogonal, and the eleven reachable ones
have total rank 858, so the bound is unsaturated with slack $\ln(3354/11)$;
\item the binary preparation reaches $1+4+11=16$ classical prefixes, so four
bits encode the complete controller history; a compact controller that reads
only this code reproduces the phase, action, forecast, source loads, record
and halt of all 264 histories; the full family reaches 24 prefixes and needs
five bits;
\item coarser retained readouts give correctable codes of size 4 for the
first letter alone, 4 for the second letter alone, 3 for the forecast alone,
2 for the chosen seam alone, 4 for forecast and seam, and 1 after complete
erasure.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Halt and reread leave the tape unchanged, so the joint kernel is the
identity and the existing evaluator returns the number of reachable terminal
words. The terminal projector of word $w$ occupies the basis coordinates
$546+78w$ to $546+78w+77$ of the carrier \cite{oCAP,rCAP}.
\end{proof}

The terminal capacity is $\ln 11$ nats, about 3.459 bits, on the binary
preparation and $\ln 17$ nats, about 4.087 bits, on the full family. The
Shannon entropy of the benchmark record law is a different quantity, about
2.023 nats. The four-bit code is the minimum fixed-width encoding of the 16
reached prefixes; a naive layout with a separate phase uses eight bits, and
an optimal index over all 43 allocated prefixes uses six.

The library's global proposal adds closure across the complete terminal
fiber, $N_\star=\ln D_\star=\ln M_0(U_{N_\star})$, and identifies the same
carrier with a de Sitter horizon through $N_\star=A_{\rm dS}/(4\ell_\star^2)$
and $A_{\rm dS}=12\pi/\Lambda$, hence $\Lambda\ell_\star^2=3\pi/N_\star$
\cite{ophobservers,oCAP}. The native records supply an actual finite input to
that evaluator. The complete-fiber closure and the horizon identification
are separate steps that this construction does not take.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$P$}{P}-dependent detector}
\label{sec:p-detector}

The detector carries the closure input $P$ through the electromagnetic
coupling into a recorded binary observation that controls the next repair
\cite{pREADME,pDERIV}. The charge assignment, probe, calibrated flux, readout
and controller are specified apparatus, and the coupling branch keeps its
documented endpoint status (Section~\ref{sec:endpoint}).

Choose the charged realization $Q_v=g(P)N_v$ of the native occupation $N_v$.
A current record $j$ on the seam $(a,b)$ then transfers the charge
$\Delta Q=g(P)\,j\,(\mathbf e_b-\mathbf e_a)$, and total charge is conserved.
For port gauge parameters $\lambda_v$ and
$U_\lambda\ket{x}=e^{ig\sum_v\lambda_vx_v}\ket{x}$, every current-grouped
Kraus operator obeys
$U_\lambda L_{(c,j)}U_\lambda^\dagger=e^{igj(\lambda_b-\lambda_a)}L_{(c,j)}$.
The probe is a unit-charge path qubit prepared in $\ket{+X}$. The first
native current routes it around a calibrated external loop with signed
exposure $j\chi$, where $\chi=\oint A\cdot dx$ is prepared independently of
$g$, so the relative phase is $\varphi_j=g(P)\,j\,\chi$, the Aharonov-Bohm
phase \cite{aharonov1959}. A $Y$ measurement commits $y=\pm1$ with amplitudes
$m_y(j)=\frac12[e^{-i\varphi_j/2}-iy\,e^{i\varphi_j/2}]$ and effects
$E_y=\sum_r\frac12[1+y\sin\varphi_{j(r)}]\proj{r}$ on the old current
register. The actuator receives only $y$. It repairs $(0,5)$ for $y=+1$ and
$(0,1)$ for $y=-1$, keeps $y$ unchanged, and the protocol halts.

\begin{proposition}[$P$-dependent response]
\label{prop:p-response}
Prepare the 66 binary configurations uniformly, run the protocol above, and
put $s=\sin(g(P)\chi)$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $\{E_y\}$ is a binary instrument on every input, including coherent
records; each $M_y=\sum_rm_y(j(r))\proj r$ commutes with the old record
projectors, and the composed experiment is one CPTP map that preserves the
committed bit;
\item $\Pr(y\mid j_1,P,\chi)=\frac12[1+y\sin(g(P)j_1\chi)]$;
\item at $\chi=1$ all 528 state, coin, detector and coin histories have
positive probability, and
\[
\Ex[y]=0,\quad \Ex[yj_1]=\tfrac{5}{33}s,\quad
\Ex[j_2]=\tfrac{5}{264}s,\quad \Ex[g(P)j_2]=\tfrac{5g(P)}{264}s;
\]
\item for weak exposure, $\Ex[g\,j_2]=\frac{5\pi}{66}\alpha(P)\chi+O(\chi^3)$;
\item disconnecting the probe, or replacing $y$ by an independent fair bit,
gives $\Ex[j_2]=0$ with the same detector and seam frequencies.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The eigenvalues of $E_y$ lie in $[0,1]$ and sum to one, which gives (i). For
(iii), after the first repair let the source be $z$; averaging the second
coin gives $\Ex[j_2\mid z,j]=\frac14(2z_0-z_1-z_5)+\frac{sj}{4}(z_1-z_5)$.
The first term averages to zero because the first repair preserves the
uniform shell. Currents $\pm1$ each have mass $5/66$, and on either branch
the other load is uniform over ten ports, so $\Ex[j(z_1-z_5)]=5/66$. An
independent floor and ceiling enumerator reproduces every coefficient over
all $66\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2=528$ paths \cite{pDERIV,pVALID}.
\end{proof}

At the computed $P$ and $\chi=1$ the plus probability is $0.649129261647$ for
$j=+1$, $0.350870738353$ for $j=-1$ and exactly $1/2$ for $j=0$. The mean
second current is $0.00564883566843$, the mean transferred charge
$0.00171084937579$ in natural units, and the equivalent source audit gives a
final port-0 load of $0.161017830998235$ against $1/6$ for the controls.
Off-closure parameter controls at $0.999P$ and $1.001P$ recompute the coupling
map and change the detector and feedback probabilities at fixed $\chi$
\cite{pREADME,rP}. Fifteen calculation checks in the receipt, six independent
tests and a fixed-seed sequential run of 200,000 trials (seed 20261012)
support the construction; the run's mean second current is $0.006390$ with
standard error $0.00085715$, consistent with the analytic value.

The actuator's record algebra is $\C(\{-1,+1\})$, with correctable size
$M_0=2$. The scientific audit retains $(r_1,y,r_2)$, with 24 terminal words, 37
prefixes, a six-bit encoding and $M_0=24$ \cite{pACCESS}. If an identified
cut enclosing a retained record obeys the area-capacity law with area equal
to $K$ chart cells, then $N_{\rm cut}=KP/4$ and $\ln M_0\le N_{\rm cut}$
give $K\ge4\ln M_0/P$. With the pinned $P$, $4\ln2/P\approx1.6999608573$ and
$4\ln24/P\approx7.7942567832$, so one retained detector bit needs $K\ge2$
and the full audit $K\ge8$ \cite{pACCESS}.

\subsection{Quantum mixing and memory}
\label{sec:mixing}

Discarding every seam, coin and repair record after each event turns the
repairs into channels $\Phi_\Cn$ and $\Phi_\En$ on the source. Under
independent seam attempts these channels have an exact spectral theory
\cite{oQM}. Theorems~\ref{thm:gap} and~\ref{thm:memory} concern this
record-discarded channel under independent uniform attempts; they do not
describe the retained tape or the feedback controller.

\begin{theorem}[Signed-difference reduction and quantum gap]
\label{thm:gap}
Let $G$ be a finite connected graph with $n\ge2$ vertices, positive edge
weights $w_{ab}$ and $W=\sum w_{ab}$. Let each event choose the edge $ab$ with
probability $w_{ab}/W$ and a fresh fair coin, and discard its record. On the
binary space $\Hil_k$ of load $k$, $1\le k\le n-1$, with $D=\binom nk$:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item for a matrix unit $\ket x\bra y$ the signed difference $z=x-y$ is
invariant; if $z$ has $r$ positive entries and $Z=\{v:z_v=0\}$, then on the
sector of $z$
\[
\Phi_{\mathsf L}\big|_z=I-\frac{L_{\rm excl}(G[Z],k-r)+K_{\mathsf L}(z)\,I}{2W},
\qquad \mathsf L\in\{\Cn,\En\},
\]
with the exclusion Laplacian $L_{\rm excl}$ of the induced graph,
$K_\Cn(z)=\sum w_{ab}\lvert z_a-z_b\rvert$ and
$K_\En(z)=2\sum w_{ab}\mathbf 1[z_a\neq z_b]$;
\item both channels are unital and Hilbert-Schmidt self-adjoint with spectrum
in $[0,1]$, their fixed algebra consists of the scalars, and both have gap
$\gamma=\lambda_1(G)/(2W)$, where $\lambda_1(G)$ is the first positive
Laplacian eigenvalue; and
$\lVert\Phi^t_{\mathsf L}-{\rm Dep}_D\rVert_{2\to2}=(1-\gamma)^t$;
\item for every input and every untouched reference $R$,
$\dist\bigl((\Phi^t_{\mathsf L}\otimes{\rm id}_R)(\rho),\tau\otimes\rho_R\bigr)
\le\min\{1,\tfrac{D}{2}(1-\gamma)^t\}$, with $\tau=I_D/D$ and
$\dist(\rho,\sigma)=\frac12\lVert\rho-\sigma\rVert_1$.
\end{enumerate}
For the twelve-port carrier at load two ($n=12$, $W=30$,
$\lambda_1=5-\sqrt5$, $D=66$) the gap is $\gamma=(5-\sqrt5)/60\approx0.046066$,
and the slowest off-diagonal sectors contract by exactly $r_\Cn=5/6$ and
$r_\En=7/10$ per attempt. On the full 78-state carrier the gap is the same,
the full channel is block triangular with a diagonal collision block whose
entries lie in $[0,5/6]$, and the unique stationary density is the uniform binary density, of rank 66 on
dimension 78.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A matrix unit survives a coin branch exactly when its two records agree, and
then both configurations translate by the same current; comparing the coin
branches gives (i). The diagonal sector is the symmetric $k$-particle
exclusion chain, whose gap equals $\lambda_1(G)$ by the theorem of Caputo,
Liggett and Richthammer \cite{caputo2010}. For a nonzero sector,
$K_\En\ge K_\Cn\ge\frac12\sum w_{ab}(z_a-z_b)^2\ge r\lambda_1\ge\lambda_1$, so
no off-diagonal mode is slower. Item (iii) uses Schmidt rank at most $D$ and
$\lVert Y\rVert_1\le D\lVert Y\rVert_2$. On the carrier, $r=1$ gives
$K_\Cn=10$ and $K_\En=2(10-\mathbf 1[uv\text{ edge}])$, while $r=2$ gives
$K_\Cn\ge16$ and $K_\En\ge24$. The collision block has largest entry
$1-d_{\min}/W=5/6\le1-\gamma$ \cite{oQM}. The executable checks
the reduction against the native primitive on all 4,356 binary matrix units
and the transient structure on all 6,084 full matrix units \cite{rQM}.
\end{proof}

Choose adjacent ports $u,v$ and the two mixtures
$\rho_\pm=\frac1{20}\sum_{w\notin\{u,v\}}(\ket{uw}\pm\ket{vw})(\bra{uw}\pm\bra{vw})$.
They have identical load populations and orthogonal supports, and
\[
\dist\bigl(\Phi^t_{\mathsf L}(\rho_+),\Phi^t_{\mathsf L}(\rho_-)\bigr)=r_{\mathsf L}^t,\qquad
\dist\bigl(\Phi^t_\Cn(\rho_+),\Phi^t_\En(\rho_+)\bigr)=\tfrac12(r_\Cn^t-r_\En^t).
\]
The second distance is $1/15$ after one attempt and peaks at four attempts at
$12259/101250$; the binary effect $\frac12(I+A_{uv}+A_{uv}^\dagger)$ with
$A_{uv}=\sum_{w\notin\{u,v\}}\ket{uw}\bra{vw}$ attains it. Load populations,
classical load histories and diagonal geometric observables obey the same
exclusion process under both laws, so only coherent preparations and readouts
distinguish them under this schedule.

\begin{theorem}[Finite quantum memory]
\label{thm:memory}
Under the schedule of Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} on the twelve-port carrier:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item on the binary shell both record-discarded channels are entanglement
breaking after at most 134 attempts;
\item on the full carrier let $u,v$ be adjacent and
$\ket\Psi=(\ket{2u}\ket0+\ket{uv}\ket1)/\sqrt2$ with a reference qubit; after
$t$ attempts the coefficient of $\ket{2u}\bra{uv}$ is $a_{\mathsf L}^t$, with
$a_\Cn=23/30$ and $a_\En=7/10$, and the partial transpose on the reference
has a principal $2\times2$ minor equal to $-a_{\mathsf L}^{2t}/4<0$; no finite
power of either full channel is entanglement breaking;
\item every input converges to $\tau_P\otimes\rho_R$, where $\tau_P$ is the
uniform binary density, with
$\dist\le\min\{1,\mu^{s/2}+\mu^s+\tfrac{D}{2}(1-\gamma)^{t-s}\}$ for all
$0\le s\le t$, $\mu=5/6$ and $D=66$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For the normalized Choi state $J_t$,
$\lVert J_t-I/D^2\rVert_2^2=D^{-2}\sum_{i>0}\lambda_i^{2t}$. The separable
ball of Gurvits and Barnum \cite{gurvits2002} and the equivalence of
separable Choi states with entanglement-breaking channels \cite{horodecki2003}
reduce (i) to $65\,r_{\rm mix}^{2t}+4290\,r_{\mathsf L}^{2t}\le1/4355$, where
$r_{\rm mix}=1-\gamma$. The rational enclosure
$559/250<\sqrt5<2237/1000$ certifies this at $t=134$ and its failure at
$t=133$; treating all modes with $r_{\rm mix}$ would certify only 178. For
(ii), 25 edges avoid $u$ and four of them touch $v$; $\Cn$ keeps one coin on
each of those four and $\En$ keeps neither, and the triangular structure
prevents any return from the binary space. Separable states have positive
partial transpose \cite{peres1996}; the complete argument is in
\cite{oQM,rQM}.
\end{proof}

Replacing the full carrier by its attracting binary shell therefore changes
an exact finite-time quantum property, although both converge to the same
stationary density. The number 134 is a sufficient certificate time; the
exact index of entanglement breaking on the shell is not determined.

\subsection{Recovery from complete records}
\label{sec:recovery}

Preserving committed records and recovering the unknown input from them are
different questions \cite{oRR}. Here a seam policy may depend on committed
records and classical randomness and may not inspect hidden loads or insert
quantum corrections; a final decoder may act on the full load register and
tape, keep the tape, use blank ancillas and return a state on the original
carrier. That decoder access is stronger than the record-only access of the
observer's controller.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal recovery from complete native records]
\label{thm:recovery}
Let $X$ be the 78 configurations or the 66-state shell and $D=\lvert X\rvert$.
For a complete admitted history $h$ let $X_h$ be its compatible inputs, $P_h$
the projector onto them, $d_h=\rk P_h$, and $w_h$ the product of its seam and
coin factors. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item the history Kraus operator is $K_h=\sqrt{w_h}\,U_hP_h$ with
$U_h^\dagger U_h=P_h$ and $\sum_hw_hP_h=I$; a tape-controlled inverse outputs
$\sum_hw_h\proj h\otimes P_h\rho P_h$, so every initial load population is
recovered;
\item among all final CPTP decoders the optimal entanglement fidelity for the
maximally mixed input is
$F_{\rm opt}=D^{-2}\sum_hw_hd_h^2=D^{-2}\sum_{x,y}H_{xy}$, with
$H_{xy}=\sum_{h:\,x,y\in X_h}w_h$;
\item for the two-event observer, $F_{\rm opt}$ equals $5543/8712$ (binary) and
$1301/2028$ (full) under $\Cn$, and $769/1452$ and $3329/6084$ under $\En$
with the same current-sign selector applied to its decoded current;
\item the maximal exactly correctable algebra on the whole input is the
commutant of $\{P_h\}$, and one event with full-support random seam choice
reduces it to the diagonal load algebra; nevertheless the qubit code
$\ket{0_L}=(\ket{\{0,2\}}+\ket{\{1,3\}})/\sqrt2$,
$\ket{1_L}=(\ket{\{0,3\}}+\ket{\{1,2\}})/\sqrt2$ satisfies the
Knill-Laflamme condition $QP_hQ=\alpha_hQ$ for every seam, coin and record of
either law and is recovered exactly after one event;
\item under independent seam attempts $H_{xy}(t)=q_{\mathsf L}(x-y)^t$, with
largest off-diagonal rates $5/6$ for $\Cn$ and $7/10$ for $\En$ on both
carriers; $F_{\rm opt}(t)\to1/D$, and neither retained instrument is
entanglement breaking at any finite horizon;
\item a fixed $\En$ seam word identifies every full-carrier input exactly
when at most one visited component has size one or two, and every binary
input exactly when at most one component is a singleton and at most one has
size two; the minimal such words have eight and seven distinct edges;
\item no bounded record-only adaptive policy makes $\Cn$ entanglement
breaking; a $\Cn$ word that doubles a minimal forest, 16 or 14 events,
excludes every nontrivial exact code; the policy that repeats each forest edge until its
coin first differs identifies the input almost surely, with stopping time
$T=m+\sum_{i=1}^mG_i$ for independent geometric $G_i$ of success probability
one half, $\Pr(T=t)=\binom{t-m-1}{m-1}2^{-(t-m)}$ for $t\ge2m$, and
$\Ex[T]=3m$, namely 24 on the full carrier and 21 on the binary shell.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Every retained current determines a translation, so reversing them gives the
input uniquely from the terminal configuration and $h$. Optimality follows
from Cauchy-Schwarz on each classical branch:
$\sum_a\lvert\Tr(R_{ha}U_hP_h)\rvert^2\le d_h^2$. For (iv), commutation is
sufficient by (i), and necessity follows from equality in the Schwarz
inequalities in a Stinespring dilation; the code has nonadjacent occupied
pairs, equal one-port means and vanishing products $N_aN_b$ on every edge,

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]

\subsection{The infinite-occupation contrast}
\label{sec:infinite}

The finite pointer measures $B_d=\mathbf 1_{(Q_d>0)}$, the sign projector of
the truncated coordinate, whereas the infinite detector multiplies by
$\mathbf 1_{x_0>0}$. Compression and spectral sign do not commute. At $d=2$,
$\mathbf 1_{(Q_2>0)}=\frac12(I+X)$ while
$V_2^*\mathbf 1_{(Q>0)}V_2=\frac12I+X/\sqrt{2\pi}$, and the two differ in norm
by $\frac12-1/\sqrt{2\pi}\approx0.10105772$ \cite{csREAD}. The finite signal
of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-contrast} is a statement about its own detector;
the infinite-occupation statement needs the infinite detector.

\begin{theorem}[Infinite-occupation feedback contrast]
\label{thm:infinite}
Take the action~\eqref{eq:common-H} on two infinite oscillator Fock spaces,
the infinite free coupled vacuum, proportional to
$\exp[-(a_0^\dagger-a_1^\dagger)^2/36]\ket{0,0}$ with squared normalization
$4\sqrt5/9$, the blank gauge code, zero native preparation current, the
matter record and feedback of Section~\ref{sec:common-construction}, and the
half-line readout of $Q_0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item the unconditional positive-coordinate probability with feedback minus
that without lies in $[-0.024045670143,\,-0.024043450955]$, and without the
interaction the contrast is exactly zero;
\item both matter records and both half-line outcomes retain their quantum
states, including the post-read infinite occupation tail;
\item along $d=2^k$, with exact palindromic factors and $n=d^2$ steps per
interval, the complete finite sign instruments converge in trace distance to
this infinite instrument, terminal states included, and the finite-family
contrast is strictly negative for all sufficiently large $d$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The linear force is relatively bounded by the positive coupled oscillator
Hamiltonian $K$, so $H$ is self-adjoint on $\mathcal D(K)$, and finite Hermite
polynomials lie in every $\mathcal D(H^k)$. Each of 24 steps of length $1/16$
applies a degree-16 Taylor polynomial of the full $H$ on expanding support,
charges its remainder $\delta^{17}\lVert H^{17}v\rVert/17!$ exactly, then
discards support above total occupation 16 and rounds coefficients to a
$2^{-112}$ grid, charging both differences; the largest expanded occupation
is 50. The complete output then has half trace-norm error at most
$\tau+\epsilon+\epsilon^2/2$ and each event at most $\tau+2\epsilon+\epsilon^2$.
Half-line probabilities have the form $r+s/\sqrt{2\pi}$ with exact rationals
$r,s$, and $\pi$ is enclosed by Machin's formula. For (iii), $A_d=P_dQ_0P_d$
converges to $Q_0$ in the strong resolvent sense on the Hermite core, the
spectral measure of $Q_0$ has no atom at zero, and $\det Q_{2m}\neq0$, so the
sign projectors converge strongly \cite{csINF,teschl2014,reedsimon1}.
\end{proof}

The $d=2$ finite continuous experiment has contrast about $-0.03166148$;
replacing only its detector by the infinite half-line effect gives about
$-0.02526221$; removing the dynamical cutoff as well gives the interval of
Theorem~\ref{thm:infinite} \cite{csINF}. The signal survives both limits,
and both approximations change its size. The interval is specific to zero
native preparation current and the declared two-site action.

\subsection{Occupation refinement and complete-output comparisons}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform pre-readout occupation bound]
\label{thm:occupation}

\subsection{Observer readouts and the fixed-reference clock}
\label{sec:readouts}

The readouts below extract observer representations, modular flow and

\begin{proposition}[Observer representations of the executed history]
\label{prop:gns}
Let $v_{ab}$ be the four unnormalized terminal vectors of the history at
$d\in\{2,4\}$ and $\rho_{\rm hist}=\bigoplus_{a,b}\proj{v_{ab}}$ on
$\bigoplus_{a,b}M_{4d^2}$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item For a positive functional $\omega$ the
Gelfand-Naimark-Segal (GNS) construction $\langle[A],[B]\rangle=\omega(A^*B)$,
$\pi(X)[A]=[XA]$, $\Omega=[I]$ has dimension $dr$ on a block $M_d$ whose
density has rank $r$. The record and work algebra has GNS dimension
$4(4d^2)$, that is 64 and 256; the four committed record projectors give a
four-dimensional classical GNS space; the site-0 observer that forgets both
records has GNS dimension $d^2$, that is 4 and 16; and the regional maps
$J_R[A]=[{\rm embed}_R(A)]$ are isometric and intertwine the regional
actions.
\item For faithful $\rho_0$ the modular group
$\sigma_s(A)=\rho_0^{is}A\rho_0^{-is}$ moves number observables; the
commutative record algebra has identity flow; states conditioned on the sharp
terminal bit are nonfaithful on the full scalar algebra, with rank at most
$d/2$.
\item At $d=2$, $\rho_0=\frac12(I-\delta X)$ with $\delta=P(b=0)-P(b=1)$ and
modular frequency $\nu=\log(P(b=0)/P(b=1))$. A source-compiled
continuation of every terminal branch by $Z(\pi/4)$, $X(s\nu/2)$ and a
fresh number pointer preserves the old two-bit record marginal and has
readout law $P(1\mid s)=\frac12(m+\delta\sin(\nu s))$, where $m$ is the
total branch mass, equal to one for the normalized history.
\item Scalar regional restrictions are completely positive conditional
expectations, normalized partial trace followed by identity embedding; they
are unital and idempotent and in general not multiplicative. The centered
components of the actual Hamiltonian give an interaction graph that is a
path through scalar site 1, scalar site 0 and the shared charged-matter and
link sector, with one edge from the scalar bond and one from the scalar and
charge interaction.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For $\rho=V\diag(\lambda)V^*$ of rank $r$ the map
$[A]\mapsto AV\sqrt{\diag(\lambda)}$ is the explicit GNS isometry
\cite{bratteli1}. The modular identities, including the finite
Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) condition, are checked with this sign convention
\cite{bratteli2}. At $d=2$, $-\log\rho_0$ equals a multiple of the identity
plus $(\nu/2)X$, and the probe implements the Schr\"odinger dual of
$\sigma_s$ \cite{csOBS}.
\end{proof}

In the executed history $\nu\approx0.5292248981159262$ and
$\delta\approx0.2586046272446501$ \cite{rBRIDGE}. Ensemble frequencies read a
modular interval on a declared monotone window, and full-cycle aliases are
indistinguishable. Taking the post-measurement site-0 state as its own
modular reference fails to reproduce the interacting dynamics. Its modular
flow preserves it, while its density changes under $H$ with derivative norms
about $0.2086$ at $d=2$ and $0.6603$ at $d=4$, so no rescaling identifies
the two flows on local expectations \cite{csCALIB}. A fixed reference on the
full work algebra removes this obstruction \cite{csTHERM}.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-reference modular relation]
\label{prop:thermal}
Let $H$ be the finite interacting Hamiltonian on the work space of dimension
$4d^2$, $\beta>0$ and $\sigma_\beta=e^{-\beta H}/\Tr e^{-\beta H}$. Then
\[
\sigma_\beta^{is}A\,\sigma_\beta^{-is}=e^{-i\beta sH}Ae^{i\beta sH}
=\alpha_{-\beta s}(A),\qquad \alpha_t(A)=e^{itH}Ae^{-itH},
\]
so continuous Hamiltonian segments correspond to modular flow at
$t=-\beta s$. For the full matrix algebra this identification of the two
automorphism groups forces the Gibbs form of the reference. With record
blocks $\Sigma_\beta=\bigoplus_rq_r\sigma_\beta$, $q_r>0$, the weights cancel
and every record projector is fixed. The actual state
$\omega(t)=e^{-itH}\omega e^{itH}$ can change while $\sigma_\beta$ is
stationary, and $\Tr(\omega(t)E)=\Tr(\omega\,\sigma_{-t/\beta}(E))$ for every
effect $E$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\log\sigma_\beta=-\beta H-\log Z_\beta\,I$, and the scalar cancels in the
conjugation. Equality of the generators requires
$[\log\sigma+\beta H,A]=0$ for every matrix $A$, and the commutant of the full
matrix algebra consists of scalars \cite{csTHERM,connesrovelli1994}.
\end{proof}

The reference, $\beta$ and the full-work access are declared choices, and
measurements and feedback act as interventions outside this one-parameter
group. The receipt uses $\beta=1$ and reference weights
$(1/10,2/10,3/10,4/10)$. Source continuations at model times $0$, $1/2$ and
$1$ append a gauge-number pointer to every post-sharp branch and retain all
eight outcomes \cite{csTHERM}.

\subsection{History repair, geometry, causal cones and field relations}
\label{sec:repair-geometry}

\begin{theorem}[Local repair of the completed history]
\label{thm:history}
Fix $d\in\{2,4\}$, the native current $j$ and the feedback setting. Let twelve
event nodes carry unnormalized work vectors subject to the compatibility
equations $x_{\rm root}={\rm prepared}(j)$, $x_1=U_1x_{\rm root}$,
$x_{(a)}=P_ax_1$, $y_{(a)}=F_ax_{(a)}$, $z_{(a)}=U_2y_{(a)}$ and
$w_{(a,b)}=B_bz_{(a)}$, of depths $0$ to $5$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item exactly one assignment satisfies all equations;
\item the common fixed set of the local updates
$x_v\leftarrow A_vx_{{\rm parent}(v)}$ equals that compatibility set;
\item every node-fair schedule reaches the compatible assignment from
arbitrary initial vectors after finitely many updates and stays there; five
covering rounds suffice, a forward topological pass needs eleven updates, and
a reverse order may need 55;
\item with subtree weights $w_v$, equal to $11$, $5$, $4$, $3$ and $1$ at
depths one to five, the potential
$\Phi=\sum_{v\neq{\rm root}}w_v\lVert x_v-A_vx_{{\rm parent}(v)}\rVert$
decreases by at least the repaired residual at every active ideal repair;
\item every deterministic extraction from the completed assignment, including
branch probabilities, terminal densities, GNS representations and modular
parameters, is independent of the schedule.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Agreement at depth zero forces agreement at each further depth. A node whose
ancestors are correct becomes correct permanently at its next selection, and
after the $k$th covering round every node of depth at most $k$ is correct.
Updating $v$ zeroes its residual and changes each child's residual by at
most $\lVert r_v\rVert$, since all maps are contractions \cite{csHIST}.
\end{proof}

The solved object is the complete branching instrument law for given
apparatus inputs; its uniqueness selects no realized record pair, and the
auxiliary solver is a host computation distinct from a native hardware
process. The geometric and field statements that follow are dimensionless
carrier displacements and event counts; physical position, momentum, length
scale and propagation speed need their own operational identification.

\begin{proposition}[Native geometric readout]
\label{prop:geometry}
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Exact enumeration of all 4,680 state, seam and coin branches gives the

\subsection{The phase experiment for two record laws}
\label{sec:ce-protocol}

The classical repair law does not select between the current instrument
$\Cn$ and the contrast instrument $\En$ (Section~\ref{sec:repair}). A fixed
interference experiment makes the choice observable, and its count protocol
is fixed before any data \cite{prREADME,prDERIV}. Both candidates are
ordinary quantum instruments, so the test separates two record laws and
makes no claim to separate OPH from quantum mechanics.

Write $x=\{2,3\}$, $z=\{1,2\}$ and $w=\{0,2\}$ for binary configurations on
the 78-dimensional carrier. Prepare
$\ket{\phi_\theta}=(\ket x+e^{i\theta}\ket z)/\sqrt2$ for
$\theta\in\{0,\pi,\pi/2\}$, or the incoherent mixture
$\frac12(\proj x+\proj z)$. Apply one repair on the seam $(0,1)$ and retain
its record. Then measure $P_+=\proj{\phi_0}$ and $P_-=I-P_+$ inside each
committed record block.

\begin{proposition}[Phase law of the two record instruments]
\label{prop:ce}
For the preparations above:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item the nonzero recorded source densities of $\Cn$ are
$\frac12\proj{\phi_\theta}$ for $(c,j)=(0,0)$, $\frac14\proj x$ for $(1,0)$
and $\frac14\proj w$ for $(1,-1)$; those of $\En$ are $\frac14\proj x$ for
$(c,d)=(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$, $\frac14\proj z$ for $(0,-1)$ and $\frac14\proj w$
for $(1,-1)$;
\item the plus probabilities are $p_\Cn(\theta)=\frac38+\frac14\cos\theta$
and $p_\En(\theta)=\frac38$, and the incoherent preparation gives $3/8$ for
both; the phase-reversal contrast is $1/2$ for $\Cn$ and zero for $\En$;
\item all four preparations have identical classical load probabilities,
the readout preserves the probability of every old record, and no record is
erased and no outcome postselected.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
On the seam $(0,1)$ the configuration $x$ has contrast zero for both coins,
and $z$ has contrast $-1$, current $0$ for coin zero and current $-1$ for
coin one, which moves it to $w$. The coherent sum survives only in the
$\Cn$ branch $(0,0)$, where both currents vanish; $\En$ separates $x$ from $z$
by their contrasts. Summing $\Tr(P_+\rho_rP_+)$ over the branches gives (ii)
\cite{prDERIV}.
\end{proof}

The count protocol fixes four settings with 8,192 fresh independent attempted
trials each and no optional stopping. Each attempt contributes to one of
twelve common bins of coin, current and outcome, or to an abort; every
frequency uses all 8,192 attempts. At each setting it scores the twelve bin
indicators, the plus indicator and the abort indicator, $m=4(12+2)=56$
statistics in all. The sampling half-width is $b=1/32$, the independently
established apparatus probability budget is $\eta=1/64$, and the familywise
sampling requirement is $\delta=0.001$.

\begin{proposition}[Sampling certificate]
\label{prop:sampling}
If the attempted trials of each setting are independent, then with $n=8192$
and $b=1/32$ the probability that any of the 56 observation intervals
$[\hat p-b,\hat p+b]$ misses its actual probability is at most
$112e^{-16}<112/S_{32}<0.000012606<\delta$, where
$S_{32}=\sum_{k=0}^{32}16^k/k!$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Hoeffding's inequality \cite{hoeffding1963} gives
$2e^{-2nb^2}=2e^{-16}$ for each statistic, and a union bound over 56
statistics gives $112e^{-16}$. Positivity of the exponential series gives
$S_{32}<e^{16}$, and the final comparison is exact rational arithmetic
\cite{prDERIV}.
\end{proof}

A candidate is rejected when some observation interval is disjoint from its
ideal probability widened by $\eta$. If the apparatus contract holds,
rejection can occur only when an observation interval fails, which proves
the familywise bound; both candidates use the same intervals, so testing
both needs no further union bound. If the calibration guarantee itself fails
with probability at most $\beta$, the combined bound is $112/S_{32}+\beta$.
An abort counts in the denominator, has ideal probability zero, and a
statistically resolved excess over its allowance returns the verdict that
the apparatus budget is exceeded. Without supplied counts the verdict is
``no data''. Software demonstrations are labelled synthetic and external
count files unverified, and every result reports empirical confirmation as
false \cite{prREADME}. If one candidate survives and the other is rejected,
the counts discriminate the conditional instruments; if both survive, the
test does not separate them; if both are rejected, neither specified
apparatus model explains the counts within the declared premises.

\subsection{The source-executed implementation}
\label{sec:ce-source}

Both complete recorded instruments, the four preparations and the readout
also run as emitted twelve-port control words \cite{sdREADME,sdDERIV}. The
encoding is a mathematical isometry; a physical interface from occupation
states to the code is not established. A configuration is written as the
seam pair and the ten spectators. The six endpoint pairs use three source
bits and the spectators six bits, which places the 78 configurations
injectively in a nine-qubit register with 434 unused addresses. A local
permutation with blank record bits realizes each repair from phase-exact
transpositions, and the fair placement coin is itself prepared by a source
pulse and recorded.

\begin{proposition}[Exact source realization of the two instruments]
\label{prop:ce-source}
In the nine-qubit encoding $J$:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item the encoded recorded channels intertwine the native ones,
\[
\mathcal E_{\rm code}(J\rho J^*)=(I_{\rm record}\otimes J)\,
\mathcal E_{\rm native}(\rho)\,(I_{\rm record}\otimes J^*),
\]
on all 6,084 matrix units of the 78-state carrier and therefore with every
untouched reference; the complete Gram identity has 156 nonzero Kraus
entries;
\item $\En$ is $\Cn$ followed by a three-pulse source controlled-NOT (CNOT) that copies the
seam-total parity $p$, with contrast decoded as $d=2j+(2c-1)p$; relative to
the retained coin and current labels one additional binary record is
necessary and sufficient, and forgetting it leaves parity dephasing without
restoring the coherence of $\Cn$;
\item the readout is the L\"uders instrument \cite{luders1950} of the
rank-one plus projector and its complement, realized by coupling the plus
projector to a blank pointer and measuring only the pointer; the minus
outcome is one rank-77 projector whose internal coherences survive, and
reading the full computational basis would implement a different
instrument.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For fixed current the endpoint map is an injective translation, and for fixed
contrast the spectators and endpoint sum reconstruct the input, so every
input column of the joint coin, record and source isometry has two entries
$2^{-1/2}$ with disjoint supports. An independent floor and ceiling
enumeration checks the permutation images and all 6,084 matrix units for each
candidate. For (ii), the inputs $\{2,3\}$ and $\{1,2\}$ share the $\Cn$
record $(0,0)$ and have contrasts $0$ and $-1$, so a single extra value cannot
separate them \cite{sdDERIV}.
\end{proof}

The complete attempted trial costs 32,006 native pulse requests for the pure
$\Cn$ settings, 32,007 for the diagonal $\Cn$ setting, 32,009 for the pure
$\En$ settings and 32,010 for the diagonal $\En$ setting, with one
proper-code load and one export charged per pulse. Each readout costs 26,632
pulses, a local $\Cn$ repair 5,370 and a local $\En$ repair 5,373; the peak
live code width is 11 qubits for $\Cn$ and 12 for $\En$
\cite{sdREADME}. Executed terminal states reproduce the probabilities of
Table~\ref{tab:ce} and the full output densities, and the synthetic counts,
8,192 attempted trials per setting drawn from executed branch norms, feed the
unchanged count protocol. For each synthetic dataset the evaluator keeps its
generating candidate and rejects the other. A compositional error contract,
\[
N_pu+N_l\,l+N_e\,e+p+z+c+a+r+s+m+g+n\le\frac1{64},
\]
with pulse, load and export counts $N_p,N_l,N_e$, their uniform channel errors
$u,l,e$, and whole-trial allowances for preparation, initialization, coin,
randomizer, records, storage, measurement, coefficient compilation and
numerical execution, gives sufficient conditions for the protocol's budget
$\eta=1/64$; an equal allocation at the largest schedule is $1/6146496$ per
term. The same source alphabet, code services and classical transitions thus
admit two implemented quantum instruments whose common observable at phase
zero differs by $1/4$, so these ingredients alone do not select the quantum
record law.

\paragraph{Complete-carrier center.} The centers of $M_{66}$ and $M_{78}$
consist of scalars, so the unextended working algebra cannot supply twelve
nonzero primitive central ports. Binary occupations are noncentral, overlap
and sum to $2I$, and total-two occupations also admit the eigenvalue two. The
native matrix algebra is therefore kept as a sector; a separate
twelve-block central-address extension supplies a central algebra with
port-controlled operations, and occupation measurement is a separate
contract.

\paragraph{Marked triplets.} The slow Gram matrix satisfies $G^2=4G$,
$\Tr G=12$ and $(5I-A)G=(5-\sqrt5)G$. One fixed order-five symmetry has trace
$(1-\sqrt5)/2$ on the slow frame and $(1+\sqrt5)/2$ on the lower response
triplet, which excludes an invertible intertwiner for the same action.
Spatial and internal response domains are therefore kept distinct; a
whole-law Galois substitution or an explicit twist of the action needs its
own attachment.

\paragraph{Feedback class.} With the two endpoint loads in the uniform
mixture of their one-particle configurations, a rational number-preserving
exchange gives endpoint probabilities $769/1250$ and $481/1250$ for the two
private preparations, a difference of $144/625$. Four Kraus operators certify that it
is CPTP, and its private commutant has dimension one, which excludes every
single-product exponential representation, alternate logarithm branches
included. The no-feedback theorems for reduced loads and single products hold
within their stated classes, and this channel marks the class boundary.

\paragraph{Configuration equilibrium.} For $A=I+L_{\rm path3}$ the controls
$\varepsilon=\pm1/16$ keep the normalized configuration Gibbs marginal and
change the full reference and the vacuum. For a stiffness response
$Y=\gamma(A+\varepsilon A^2)$ one has $Y_{13}=\gamma\varepsilon$ and a
third-mode fit residual $6\gamma\varepsilon$, while the actual acceleration is
$-Yq$. The theorem that identifies inertia with the same capacity keeps that
premise, and equal equilibrium configurations do not select the kinetic law.

\section{Open connections}
\label{sec:open}

The finite components above are completed under their contracts. The
connections below join them into the target of Section~\ref{sec:target}; they
can advance in parallel, and none requires a uniqueness theorem for the
chosen design.

\paragraph{A spatially refining family.} A family of source programs that
refines in space, carries the charges, interactions and instruments of
Section~\ref{sec:common}, retains its records, and bounds numerical and
physical services along the way would connect the construction to a
continuum. Two oscillators at increasing occupation cutoff stay two
oscillators, and the spatial bounds of Section~\ref{sec:scalar} transfer to
another implementation only through an explicit map. No continuum limit of
the interacting family is established.

\paragraph{Matter, geometry and clocks on one process.} Interacting matter,
responsive geometry and a physical clock calibration have to attach to the
same state, event law and retained records. This needs stress and geometric
readouts from the same family, the balance and Ward identities with
controlled tails, a calibrated scale, a choice of modular reference produced
by the construction, and a relation between modular readings and causal
depth. The calibration examples show that fixing the speed of light relates
length and time units and leaves an energy scale free \cite{csCALIB}.

\paragraph{Physical realization of the phase test.} The test of
Section~\ref{sec:prediction} needs a realized preparation and observable, an
independently established service budget, and complete records collected
under the fixed protocol. A physical model of nature additionally needs
sector-specific predictions; comparisons with data seen before a protocol was
fixed are postdictions.

\paragraph{Joins between executed components.} Several certified
components run on separate schedules with separate error budgets. The
source-executed record instruments of Section~\ref{sec:ce-source} run apart
from the common source of Section~\ref{sec:common}, whose native step is the
declared primitive; the two-read field history of
Section~\ref{sec:two-read} has no source compilation; the local split-update
engine and the two-read continuation of Section~\ref{sec:fields} carry
different bounds; and the modular and fixed-reference clocks of
Section~\ref{sec:readouts} continue the finite sharp history without an
occupation limit of their own. Each join needs its own implementation and
error analysis, and certified numerical execution together with independently
established physical service bounds is a separate budget for every executed word. The smooth and sharp common-source instruments are compiled only at $d=2$ and $d=4$; larger cutoffs are analytic comparisons.

\paragraph{Longer observers.} Observers with explicit memory, reset,
continuation and resource contracts should preserve the demonstrated
behaviour of the two-event components. The incident-tick memory test becomes
an implemented apparatus once the N4 preparation, occupation detector and
local event counter are joined; and a native quantum realization of the
global history repair of Section~\ref{sec:repair-geometry} is a separate
construction requirement.
