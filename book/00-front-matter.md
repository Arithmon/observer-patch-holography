## How to read the claims

This book develops OPH's proposed ontology: a complete self-consistent history and the computation carried by its own local interactions are one reality. No external computer or extra time is needed to play that history. Internal events, changing local states and records belong to the structure itself. A complete quantum history need not assign simultaneous outcomes to incompatible unperformed measurements. A closure condition on the whole history does not say that its inhabitants approach a final instantaneous state.

Space, physical durations, fields and matter are intended as emergent readouts. A graph, coordinate system, action, carrier or clock supplied to a particular construction nevertheless remains one of that construction's premises. The finite examples and conditional theorems in these chapters have different models and hypotheses; together they do not yet establish a common physically correct microscopic law. Historical numbers retain their producing models and calibration, rather than becoming independent predictions by appearing in one narrative.

Locality does not forbid work counters, conventional orderings or global calculations used to check a model. It forbids importing undeclared information into the local update rule. No preferred universal physical clock is assumed; comparing an update count, causal order, coordinate parameter, modular parameter or operational clock requires an explicit map. An observer in an engineering theorem need not be conscious. The claims about experience and ethics in the final chapters add philosophical premises that numerical agreement alone does not supply.

Appendix B gives the working definitions. The maintained [OPH interpretation and working terms](https://github.com/FloatingPragma/observer-patch-holography/blob/main/docs/INTERPRETATION_AND_TERMS.md) states the same boundaries alongside the current papers.
