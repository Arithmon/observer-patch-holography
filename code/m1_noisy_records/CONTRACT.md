# Noisy source records: objective, deliverables and exit

## Objective

Remove reliable run-time classical records and feedback from the constructed
fixed-rate source realization. Preserve its complete prepared read histories,
including aborts, with noisy storage, decisions and public record maintenance.
Use the existing proper-code/CCG capability, not a new reliable controller axiom.

## Deliverables

1. An exact causal instrument compiler, with independent full-channel checks
   of adaptive measurements, Boolean decisions, feedback and retained aborts.
2. A concrete classical archive circuit, including every live-wire idle,
   arbitrary-size fault proof and exhaustive small-instance adversarial replay.
3. A protected-to-public record interface that does not encode an already
   corrupted bare bit or broadcast an unprotected decision to an entire block.
4. A credited measurement-free universal protection construction, a charged
   source schedule, and all-size noise, history and accounting bounds. Count
   maintenance over each record's entire lifetime and the fresh blank supply.
   Specify a computation code and prove its distance exceeds the complete
   correction-and-gate spread, rather than transferring the smaller old code.
5. Explicit terminal-output and unmaintained-history counterbounds; strict
   independent evidence replay, hostile mutations, claim registration and CI.

## Exit

For every fixed finite quantum dephasing and local classical bit-fault rate,
construct a cofinal refinement with convergent decoded quantum outputs and
the joint public coded history. The physical archive must consist of actual
noisy classical bits, not only a private quantum record reclassified as public.
The finite experiments must retain all outcomes and the source causal clock.

The source completion, local-noise model, static external schedule and cofinal
control/inventory capability remain declared. No numerical full-machine
threshold, fault-free bare output bit, arbitrary unknown raw-input encoder,
physical source selection or laboratory energy calibration is asserted.
