# Read-law selection: objective, deliverables and exit

## Objective

Determine when constrained finite classical A3 selection can guarantee a
remote read, connect that criterion to actual scalar means and interventions,
and distinguish complete-history entropy from a merely state-determining
observer cover. Establish what this route forces and what constraints or
cover-selection evidence it needs. The full M1
objective remains deriving the metric-neighbour read law from the complete
A1--A3 architecture; this bounded result does not replace that objective.

## Deliverables

1. Lean proofs connecting full-support selection to wordwise correctness,
   counting the exact mass of subalphabet words, and proving that repairs
   avoiding a source cut cannot communicate an intervention outside it.
2. An all-finite-horizon obstruction for any local decoder, even if the
   decoder knows the whole word. The word, initial exterior, and decoder
   metadata must be the same for both source interventions. Derive the
   two-input error bound; do not claim that each input has that error rate.
3. A pinned census of every distinct undirected scalar seam in the existing
   W12 level-three capture, its source-carrier cut, and exact rational bounds
   for horizons 0, 1, 2, 16, 256 and 2279. No regular-degree assumption.
4. Independently replay all words of a small three-edge chain for horizons
   zero through six, with two source interventions, all intermediate means
   and exact errors counted. Check explicit supported positive and negative
   routing controls on the full captured graph.
5. Strict independent verification, source and proof pins, adversarial
   resealed mutations, real CLI failures, and a transitive standard-axiom gate.
6. Derive the support theorem for every attained weighted classical KL
   minimum over an arbitrary convex nonnegative feasible family, with
   positive weights and faithful reference. Do not assume IID, uniformity,
   a full simplex, reference feasibility, a KKT oracle or a sampled minimum.
7. Derive the zero-failure criterion for directly scored atoms and for
   nonnegative observables of those atoms, and connect it to the native cut
   obstruction for correlated constrained finite schedule families.
8. Kernel-check an exact nonuniform correlated optimizer over its entire
   affine family, and two distinct unique optimizers for the same convex
   history family and faithful reference under two state-determining covers.
   The latter must demonstrate why injectivity alone does not justify
   promoting an atom-support result to every inferred history observable.
9. Independently verify rational optimizer and cover certificates, reject
   feasible-but-nonoptimal candidates, and retain nonconvex and assumed-success
   face controls showing the role of each hypothesis and added constraint.

## Exit

All theorem declarations compile without sorry, compiler trust or project
axioms. The independent verifier reconstructs the entire seam census and
every finite control. It rejects malformed evidence, changed alphabet,
cut, horizon, payload, decoder, histories, probability bounds or source
identity. The package tests and repository gates pass. Existing
proofs, evidence and frozen tests remain unchanged.

## Assumptions and boundary

The schedule result consumes `RepairWordSchedule.FullWordKLProjection`:
complete quotient event identities, the full finite-word PMF simplex feasible,
and KL minimization to uniform source counting. Current A1--A3 do not supply
these premises. For the captured calculation, the candidate alphabet is one
pair mean per distinct undirected captured seam, with repetitions and waits
enabled. Its completeness as an A1-generated event grammar is not derived.

The receiver is exterior to carrier 0. Only that carrier's initial data
changes under intervention. There is no payload-dependent schedule, remote
metadata, preparation of the answer, or side channel. An independent balanced
choice of the two payloads has error at least half the cut-avoidance mass;
this is an average lower bound and hence a worst-input lower bound. Cutting
the boundary at least once is necessary, not sufficient, for delivery.

This result excludes certainty at a prescribed finite deadline for this
unconstrained schedule model. It does not exclude high-probability service,
unbounded stopping times, source-derived temporal constraints, another
reference, or an independently justified accepted-record policy. Selecting
successful histories changes the feasible schedule problem; their discarded
attempts and acceptance mechanism cannot disappear from M1 accounting.

The constrained theorem removes the full-simplex and uniform-word hypotheses
for the finite classical KL branch. It is conditional on the actual feasible
family, faithful reference and positive entropy-cover weights; their source
derivation is not supplied. Flattened classical cover atoms obey the support
theorem, but arbitrary reconstructed history events need a nonnegative
readout representation to inherit it. Injectivity of a cover on the feasible
family does not provide that representation. The same-reference cover control
proves this boundary. No general noncommutative A3 selection theorem is claimed.

No complete A1--A3 countermodel, full native q=13/q=21 history, arbitrary
record compiler or source-selected controller is supplied. No canonical
axiom is amended, no missing premise is adopted, and M1 is not derived.
