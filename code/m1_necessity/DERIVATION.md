# Which parts of M1 survive a change of read graph?

## Explicit families

Fix L,c>0. For each integer n>=1 set R=2^n, q=R^2, h=L/q and
delta=hR/c=L/(cR). The q^3 sites are h*z with z in {0,...,q-1}^3.
The event (j,z) has time j*delta and weight delta*h^3. All read edges go
from one layer to the next. A symmetric integer stencil V containing 0
specifies the parents: z reads z-v for every v in V for which both sites
are in the box. The order is generated by these actual edges.

The dense stencil B contains every integer v with |v|_2<=R. Define S by:

* for every integer w with |w|_infinity=n, include v(w), whose coordinate
  i is sign(w_i)*floor(R*|w_i|/sqrt(sum w_j^2));
* include every +/-2^b e_i, for i=1,2,3 and 0<=b<=n;
* include 0, and remove duplicate vectors.

Define A=S union {+/-j e_i: 1<=j<=R, i=1,2,3}. These are explicit finite
sets, invariant under coordinate permutations and sign changes. Every vector
has norm at most R, so S subset A subset B. The number of face samples is
(2n+1)^3-(2n-1)^3=24n^2+2. Therefore

    |S| <= D_n := 24n^2+6n+9,       |A| <= D_n+6R.

Every event, edge, clock and weight is defined before any outcome is tested.
The same events, positions and weights are used for all three families.
They are mathematical regulators, not claimed native OPH sources.

## A constructive path theorem

Let d be any nonzero integer displacement, r=|d|_2, u=d/r and M=|d|_infinity.
Round each coordinate of n*d/M to its nearest integer, with half ties away
from zero, to obtain w. At least one coordinate has absolute value n.
Write z=d/M. Then |z|_2>=1 and |w/n-z|_2<=sqrt(3)/(2n). For nonzero a,b,

    |a/|a| - b/|b|| <= 2|a-b|/|b|.

This follows by adding and subtracting a/|b| and using the reverse triangle
inequality. Apply it to a=w/n, b=z. Rounding the normalized flight towards
zero adds at most sqrt(3) in Euclidean norm, hence

    |v(w)-R*u|_1 <= 3R/n+3.                         (1)

Take k=floor(r/R) copies of v(w). The residual e=d-k*v(w) is integral and

    |e|_1 <= 3r/n+3r/R+2R =: E_n(r),                (2)

because |d-kR*u|_1<sqrt(3)R<2R and k<=r/R. For each coordinate e_i, use
floor(|e_i|/R) signed R-steps and then the binary digits of the remainder.
There are at most n remainder steps per coordinate. This constructs a path
of N steps with exact endpoint d and

    r/R <= N <= (1+3/n+3/R)*r/R+3n+2.              (3)

The lower bound is the triangle inequality. The upper bound follows from
N<=k+|e|_1/R+3n. Zero displacement uses no steps; any desired extra integer
duration is supplied by the zero vector. The executable route implements
this construction and charges every repeated and binary-correction step.

There is also a boundary bound, not just an endpoint estimate. During the
first k steps, the route differs from the point mR*u of the straight segment
by at most k*(3R/n+3) in l1 norm. During the axis corrections each coordinate
approaches its target monotonically, so its l1 distance to the endpoint is
at most |e|_1. The whole path stays within distance E_n(r) of the segment.
Thus it is a path in the finite box whenever this tube fits in the box.

In physical units, (3) gives

    distance/c <= N*delta
      <= (1+3/n+3/R)*distance/c + (3n+2)*L/(cR).     (4)

The tube thickness is at most (3/n+3/R)*distance+2L/R. Both excess duration
and thickness tend to zero uniformly for bounded displacements. This is an
all-n constructive theorem, not an extrapolation of a finite graph census.

## Causal/count, interval and clock replacement theorem

For any fixed interior spacetime pair with time difference greater than
Euclidean distance/c, its assigned grid events are eventually connected in
S: (4) fits in the strict time margin, the tube fits in the convex box, and
zero steps fill the remaining layer gap. Grid assignment errors are at most
sqrt(3)h spatially and delta temporally. A spacelike pair is never connected
once its margin exceeds these errors, since each step has norm<=R. Backward
time pairs are excluded by layer ordering. The same conclusions hold for A
and B by S subset A subset B and the shared outer bound.

Assign each spatial point to its half-open h-cube and each time to its
half-open delta interval. These cells partition the window, up to null
boundaries, and each has exactly weight delta*h^3. Their diameters vanish.
Consequently fixed boundary-null region counts converge to their spacetime
volumes. This count statement is common to all three families even at finite
n, since the event population and weight are identical.

For generated intervals with converging interior tips, the same strict-margin
argument works for every point off the two limiting null boundaries. If the
full limiting diamond is contained in the window, the selected cell indicators
converge almost everywhere to its indicator. They are bounded by one on a
common bounded time-space box. Dominated convergence therefore proves actual
generated-interval count convergence, not merely counts in a separately chosen
coordinate region. For two event variables, the only additional exceptional
sets are the pair null relation and the diagonal; these have zero product
measure. Applying the same argument to product cells proves strict-pair count
convergence. These are the same null-set and volume integrals established in
`SourceCausalBoundary`, `FlatDiamondVolume`, `FlatDiamondPairIntegral` and
`FlatDiamondNormalization`, applied here after proving the required
indicator convergence for S and A rather than assuming an all-neighbor path.

An interior timelike diamond of proper duration T has volume pi*c^3*T^4/24.
The strict comparable ordered-pair volume is V^2/20; thus counting each
comparable unordered pair once gives 2*P/[N(N-1)] -> 1/10. For two nondegenerate
diamonds the fourth root of their event-count ratio tends to their proper-time
ratio. This is a retrospective count readout, not a constructed physical clock.

The positive conformal reweighting sigma(j*delta)^4 also transfers: for any
bounded continuous positive profile, weighted cell indicators converge to
sigma(t)^4 dt dx. Finite mass identities and extrema bounds require only the
event weights, not complete-neighbor reads. The edges in comoving labels do
not change under positive rescaling of the positions and displacement stencil
at the receiving layer. Consequently the existing FLRW mass identity and
constant-profile count-ratio identities apply to S and A; they do not select
the profile, a Friedmann equation, a physical redshift instrument or curvature.

Thus complete-neighbor reads are unnecessary for these named kinematic and
measure conclusions. The new families need O(n^2) or O(R+n^2) parents per
event, versus Theta(R^3) for B. Over horizon L/c, with R transitions and q^3
sites, S has at most q^3*R*D_n=O(q^(7/2)log(q)^2) read incidences; B has
Theta(q^5). These are incidences, not packet bits or native work. Finite M1
truth tables, all independent dense input tuples and exact finite orders are
not preserved by this replacement.

## A cut observable that the replacement does not preserve

Use index coordinates. The coordinate cut is x=(q-1)/2; q is even, so no site
lies on it. Each symmetric stencil vector v with v_x>0 contributes exactly

    v_x*(q-|v_y|)*(q-|v_z|)                         (5)

unordered crossing pairs, since v_x<=R<=q/2. Count each pair once; actual
ordered layer reads in the two directions have twice this count. For S,

    X_S <= D_n*R*q^2.                              (6)

For B, retain only 1<=v_x<=R/2 and |v_y|,|v_z|<=R/2. These vectors are strictly
inside the radius ball. Using q-|v_i|>=q/2 gives

    X_B >= R^4*q^2/32,   hence X_S/X_B<=32D_n/R^3 ->0.  (7)

The existing cap-volume calculation gives the sharper X_B~pi*R^4*q^2/4.
Our separation needs only the elementary lower bound (7). Equal limiting
causal order and count-volume therefore do not determine crossing-read entropy
under the existing one-nat-per-crossing-read dictionary. The coefficient can
change by an unbounded factor without changing the named spacetime limits.

## Orientation dependence survives any single scalar calibration

The A family strengthens the separation. Let X_0 be the crossing count of its
full axial subset at the coordinate cut, and Y_0 the axial count at the plane
x+y=q-1/2. The latter cut also contains no lattice sites. Direct enumeration
gives

    X_0 = q^2*R(R+1)/2,
    Y_0 = q^2*R(R+1)-q*R(R+1)(2R+1)/3.             (8)

For a positive x step j, the coordinate cut has j*q^2 pairs. At the diagonal
cut the source sums x+y are q-j,...,q-1; each admits q-j allowed (x,y) pairs
after imposing the translated box boundary. Thus each of the x and y axes
contributes q*j*(q-j), and summing proves (8). Z steps do not cross either
cut. These counts include finite box corrections, not just infinite-plane
approximations.

The extra S edges add 0<=E_x<=D_n*R*q^2 and 0<=E_d<=2D_n*R*q^2. For the
diagonal bound, each v crosses at most |v_x+v_y|<=2R integer sum levels,
each with at most q^2 allowed sites. Put b=1-(2R+1)/(3q). Then

    b/(1+2D_n/R) <= X_A(diagonal)/(2X_A(coordinate))
                       <= b+2D_n/R.               (9)

Here D_n/R->0 and q=R^2, so the ratio in (9) tends to one. The coordinate
section has physical area L^2; the diagonal section of [0,L]^3 at this offset
has area sqrt(2)*(1-1/(2q))*L^2. Hence

    [X_A(diagonal)/area(diagonal)] /
    [X_A(coordinate)/area(coordinate)] -> sqrt(2),  (10)

whereas B's complete-neighbor law has limit one by its rotation-independent
cap integral. A still has the same round causal/count limit and exact cubic
stencil symmetry. A scalar conversion from crossings to entropy or Planck area
cancels in (10), so it cannot restore orientation independence. This is a
finite-construction counterexample to inferring isotropic cut entropy from
those kinematic premises, not a countermodel to all OPH axioms or to RG.

## A sparse positive scalar action on the same spatial family

There is a further positive replacement, with an explicit timing boundary.
For V=S,A or B remove 0 and set M2=sum_(v in V)|v|^2. It is positive because
the unit coordinate steps occur. Sign changes kill every mixed second moment;
coordinate permutations equate the diagonal moments. Therefore

    sum v_i*v_j = (M2/3)*delta_ij.

Put alpha=6/(h^2*M2) and define

    (A_V f)(x) = alpha*sum_(v in V)(f(x)-f(x+h*v)).  (11)

Outside the finite box use the fixed value zero. The matrix is real symmetric
and positive semidefinite: its energy (1/2)<f,A_V f>_h is alpha*h^3/4 times the sum
of squared differences over internal directed edges, plus alpha*h^3/2 times
the squared values for each missing outgoing boundary neighbor. All coefficients
and boundary terms are specified; no continuum fit selects them.

For a C^4 function on R^3, with unit-direction fourth derivative bounded by M4
on the required segments, Taylor expansion and the exact second moments give

    |A_V f(x)+Delta f(x)| <= a^2*M4/4,  a=hR.       (12)

Odd terms cancel between v and -v. Each fourth-order remainder is bounded by
h^4*|v|^4*M4/24; summing and using |v|^4<=R^2*|v|^2 gives (12). This applies
at every site if the continuum reference is zero in a neighborhood of the
boundary (or with periodic smooth data and periodic boundaries). The error
vanishes with the very same a=L/sqrt(q). Unlike the generic Voronoi-kernel
quadrature estimate, this explicit normalized stencil does not need
h/a^2->0. It resolves that particular spatial consistency restriction by
using exact discrete moments; it does not prove a new native force law.

For a continuum solution f_tt-c^2 Delta f+m^2 f=0 that stays smoothly supported
away from the boundary up to time T, with M4 uniform over that time interval,
compare its grid samples with the exact
finite system F_tt+(c^2 A_V+m^2 I)F=0 initialized with those samples and their
velocities. The operator is nonnegative, so
|sin(t sqrt(B))/sqrt(B)|<=t, with its continuous value t on zero modes.
Duhamel and (12), in the discrete norm |u|_h^2=h^3 sum|u_x|^2, yield

    |F(t)-f(t)|_h <= c^2*t^2*a^2*L^(3/2)*M4/8.     (13)

A detector h^3 sum g_x F_x with uniformly bounded discrete norm of g obeys
the same error times |g|_h. Thus these explicit sparse positive actions share
the smooth free-field/detector limit, including vanishing readout outside the
continuum wave support. This is finite-time convergence to a supplied smooth
reference, not an interacting quantum theorem, preparation selection, exact
finite causal support or a native implementation of the evolution operator.

### The M1 tick cannot be silently reused for centered leapfrog

Consider nonnegative symmetric translation-invariant weights w_v on a periodic
box, with displacements nonzero modulo the box, |h*v|<=a, and exact isotropic normalization
sum w_v(h*v)_i(h*v)_j=2 delta_ij. Taking the trace gives
sum w_v|h*v|^2=6, so sum w_v>=6/a^2. The Laplacian matrix has constant
diagonal sum w_v, hence its largest eigenvalue is at least 6/a^2. Centered
leapfrog with field speed c is stable only if c^2 dt^2 lambda_max<=4
(adding nonnegative mass cannot help). If dt=a/c, its left side is at least
6. Therefore this direct use of the M1 tick is unstable for every member of
this positive isotropically normalized class, including the dense family.

This is a precise integration obstruction, not an inconsistency of M1 or of
all time integrators. Exact semidiscrete evolution in (13) remains defined.
Substeps, another integrator or a different action require their own native
operation/clock accounting; faster computational substeps cannot silently be
called physical reads with the original speed limit. The preserved spatial
action result and the unsupplied physical execution join must stay distinct.

## The square-root radius is not selected by these conclusions

The same construction gives an explicit alternative radius schedule, not just
a freedom to assume another successful limit. For any fixed integer k>=2,
keep R=2^n and the exact stencils S,A,B, but set q=R^k, h=L/q and
delta=hR/c. All finite route and cut formulas above still hold: they require
only R<=q/2. Equation (4)'s last term becomes (3n+2)*L*R/(c*q), and its
tube thickness becomes (3/n+3/R)*distance+2*L*R/q. Both vanish for every
such k. The same cell, interval, pair and clock proof therefore applies.
The positive-action error is O((L*R/q)^2), which also vanishes. In (9),
b still tends to one and D_n/R still tends to zero; the entropy anisotropy
survives this change of refinement schedule.

Thus the explicit radii a=L*q^(-(k-1)/k), including both L/sqrt(q) and
L*q^(-2/3), preserve all the named limits and the cut counterexample. The
sites, ticks and distinguished-event density q^3/(L^3*delta) are specified
anew for each schedule; their equal finite counts are asserted only between
S,A,B at a fixed schedule. This is not a theorem that arbitrary populations
or radii work, or that native source histories refine compatibly. It proves
that the preserved limiting observables do not uniquely require the M1
square-root radius, Fibonacci population, or complete-neighbor law.

## What the necessity result decides

An exact one-layer M1 read cannot simply be deleted while retaining its finite
order: no path through two or more edges connects adjacent layers. With
independently variable parent records, omitting a parent also changes a
lossless tuple read; two preparations differing only there give the same
remaining inputs. Our positive theorem concerns the named limiting observables,
and the cut counterexample explains why that scope cannot be silently enlarged.

Golden quadrature and the historical native routing receipts remain valid
theorems about their own constructions. Their exact finite values and counts
do not transfer. A source-selected action, quantum instruments, full physical
causal identification, laboratory calibration and matter/current coupling
also do not follow from the preserved kinematics. The consumer inventory
records these distinctions without closing physical premises.

Sparse geometric approximation is a standard idea (for example, Kanj and
Perkovic, https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.2864). Their planar weighted spanner theorem
is not used here: our three-dimensional integer paths pay one full tick per
edge and explicitly charge the correction overhead. Likewise these preferred-
frame finite graphs do not evade the obstruction to equivariant finite-valency
graphs on Lorentz-invariant sprinklings proved by Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin,
https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0605006. Only the stated continuum observables have
the Lorentzian limit; no exact finite-history boost symmetry is claimed.
