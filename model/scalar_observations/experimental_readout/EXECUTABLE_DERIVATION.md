# Occupation control for an executed oscillator readout

This certificate concerns a specific supplied single-mode preparation and
measurement. The source implementation executes a thermal code preparation,
a displacement, a number rotation, and the complete smooth pointer dilation.
Both pointer outcomes are retained. The reference is the corresponding
unrestricted oscillator operation. Neither an experimental calibration error
nor agreement with the laboratory apparatus follows from this comparison.

The nearest existing result is the projected-path occupation argument in the
[source scalar cutoff certificate](../../observer/source_scalar/cutoff.py).
The monitored projection residual in
[Woods, Cramer and Plenio, *Simulating Bosonic Baths with Error Bars*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.01531)
is a related method. Here we specialize the argument to a displaced thermal
preparation, its actual capped finite implementation, and the two coherent
arms of the detector. We derive the needed moment and residual bounds below;
no general interacting-bath theorem is imported without its hypotheses.

## Declared preparations and operations

Let `[a,a†]=1`, `N=a†a`, and `P_d=sum_{n=0}^{d-1}|n><n|`. Write
`a_d=P_d a P_d` and `D(z)=exp(z a†-z* a)`. The full thermal state with mean
occupation `nbar>=0` has weights

```text
r = nbar/(1+nbar),             p_n=(1-r)r^n.
```

The finite source prepares the capped distribution

```text
p_n^cap=(1-r)r^n   (0<=n<d-1),       p_{d-1}^cap=r^(d-1).
```

These weights sum to one. There is no preparation rejection or conditioning.
Compared with the full thermal distribution, precisely the probability mass
`r^d` has moved from levels `n>=d` to level `d-1`. Its initial trace distance
is exactly `r^d`.

The finite displacement is
`D_d(alpha)=exp(alpha a_d†-alpha* a_d)`. It is not assumed equal to
`P_d D(alpha) P_d`. The detector at characteristic coordinate `beta` uses
`G_beta=-i(beta a†-beta* a)` and

```text
K_0=cos((G_beta+theta)/2),      K_1=sin((G_beta+theta)/2).
```

The finite detector replaces `G_beta` by `P_d G_beta P_d` before taking the
functions. Its pointer dilation is generated by `(G_beta+theta) tensor Y/2`.
In the pointer's `Y` basis its field branches are `D(+beta/2)` and
`D(-beta/2)`, up to pointer-only phases and sign convention. A blank pointer
has equal squared weight in the two arms. Number rotations commute with
`P_d`, so implementing a rotated quadrature through a number rotation creates
no additional occupation term. Pointer-only operations also commute with it.

The implementation leaves the field in the rotated number frame after the
read. The terminal-field comparison uses that same frame on both sides;
equivalently, apply the same inverse number rotation to both outputs. This
common unitary changes neither trace distance nor pointer probabilities.

Here `theta=0` reads `Re chi(beta)` and `theta=-pi/2` reads `Im chi(beta)`.
The reference characteristic function

```text
chi(beta)=exp(-(nbar+1/2)|beta|^2
              + beta alpha* - beta* alpha)
```

is an independent mathematical diagnostic. The numerical predictions in the
executable comparison must come from the contracted source operation and
its Born probabilities, not from substituting this formula for execution.
The occupation proof does use the analytic reference state to bound errors.

## A positive exponential number moment

For `q>1` and `h=1-nbar(q-1)>0`, a displaced thermal state satisfies

```text
M(q,z) = Tr[D(z) rho_thermal D(z)† q^N]
       = exp((q-1)|z|^2/h)/h.                         (1)
```

For vacuum, the coherent-state number distribution is Poisson, which gives
(1) with `nbar=0`. For `nbar>0`, write the thermal state as the positive
coherent-state mixture with density `exp(-|w|^2/nbar)/(pi nbar)`.
Each coherent state contributes `exp((q-1)|w+z|^2)`. Completing the two real
Gaussian squares gives (1); convergence is exactly `h>0`.

Consequently both `Pr(N>=d)` and `Pr(N=d)` are bounded by `M(q,z)/q^d`.
The moment increases with `|z|^2`. A rational upper bound on the squared
displacement therefore controls the entire declared path, not only sampled
points on that path. The implementation evaluates the exponential with a
positive Taylor series and a bounded geometric remainder, rounded upward.
Square roots are also directed rational bounds.

The preparation path is `z(s)=s alpha`, `0<=s<=1`, so its squared radius is
bounded by `A=|alpha|^2`. Along either pointer arm,
`z(s)=alpha +/- s beta/2`. Its squared norm is a convex quadratic in `s`.
Taking the larger endpoint and both signs gives the exact common bound

```text
B = |alpha|^2 + |beta|^2/4 + |Re(alpha* beta)|.        (2)
```

The function `log(M(q,z)/q^d)` has increasing derivative
`|z|^2/h^2+nbar/h-d/q`. The implementation uses its exact rational sign to
select neighboring points on a rational grid. This is solely a tightening
step: every admissible individual weight already supplies a valid bound.
Let `T_A` and `T_B` be the resulting tail bounds, each intersected with the
trivial bound one. At zero displacement, the exact geometric tail may
further tighten the result.

## The complete finite-code path

Split the initial full thermal state into the common subnormalized mixture
of levels `n<d`, of trace `s^2=1-r^d`, and the remaining tail. The capped
finite state shares exactly that common mixture and adds mass `r^d` at
level `d-1`. The two remaining positive states have equal trace `r^d`;
after arbitrary respective unitary dilations their contribution to trace
distance is at most `r^d`.

Purify the common low-level mixture with an untouched reference. Let `psi`
be its unrestricted path and `phi_d` its finite path. Both have norm `s`.
Initially they agree and lie in `P_d`. Since the full thermal mixture
dominates the common mixture as a positive operator, (1) bounds its displaced
occupation probabilities as well; no renormalization or division by `s`
is needed.

For one segment generated by `z a†-z* a`, the projected full path obeys the
finite equation with a boundary residual. Only the lowering transition
`|d> -> |d-1>` contributes to `P_d G (1-P_d)`. Therefore its norm is at most
`|z| sqrt(d Pr(N=d))`. Duhamel's formula and unitarity of the finite
propagator give the preparation and pointer residual bounds

```text
R_A = |alpha| sqrt(d T_A),
R_B = |beta| sqrt(d T_B)/2.
```

For the pointer, the two `Y` arms are orthogonal and each has squared weight
one half. A common upper bound for both arms is therefore also a bound for
the full vector; no additional factor two is necessary. Coherent pointer
rotations preserve its norm. The projected final difference is at most
`R=R_A+R_B`. The unrestricted outside component is orthogonal to that
projected difference and has squared norm at most `T_B`. It follows that

```text
||phi_d-psi|| <= sqrt(R^2+T_B) = epsilon.             (3)
```

For two pure vectors of the same norm `s`, the trace distance of their
unnormalized projectors is at most `s` times their vector distance. This
follows from
`s^4-|<phi_d,psi>|^2 <= s^2 ||phi_d-psi||^2`.
Tracing out the purifying reference and measuring the pointer can only
decrease trace distance. Combining the common part with the omitted initial
tail gives the final certificate

```text
Delta_d <= min(1, r^d + sqrt(1-r^d) sqrt((R_A+R_B)^2+T_B)).  (4)
```

This bounds the complete classical pointer record and terminal quantum field
state. In particular it bounds the absolute error of either binary outcome
probability; a spin mean has at most twice that error. The formulas for the
two phases share the same bound. At `beta=0` the outcome probabilities are
pointer-only identities, so their occupation error is exactly zero even
though the terminal field state may still differ.

## Scope of the certificate

The [implementation](executable_bounds.py) accepts exact rational declared
parameters and returns every bound as a rational number. A floating supplied
parameter can be converted to its exact binary rational value before calling
it. This binds the theorem to that supplied point; it does not cover the
statistical uncertainty of estimating the point from data.

The source compiler's finite-matrix contraction and independent target
residual are separate numerical checks. Computing the capped thermal weights
with floating arithmetic also belongs to numerical implementation error;
(4) concerns the exact geometric weights at the supplied rational parameter.
Neither those diagnostics nor (4)
bound real apparatus service errors. The laboratory's preparation selection,
coordinate ambiguity and aggregate-shot limitations remain in force.
There is no intervening interacting evolution in this component, so this
result identifies neither an action nor a physical clock.

## Long source-word contraction

The dense 64-level basis and displacement words each contain more than a
million native pulses. Repeated floating evaluation can accumulate radial
drift beyond the existing whole-matrix polar guard, even when the complete
phase-sensitive target residual remains small. The readout implementation
therefore groups each original word into fixed chronological blocks of at
most 4,096 pulses. This grouping is chosen before inspecting any residual.
Every block is executed by the unchanged native executor and passed through
the unchanged polar guards; no pulse, scalar phase or proper-code service
is removed. The original full-word hash and resource counts remain binding.

Write `R_k` for the raw executed block and `Q_k` for its guarded numerical
representation. Both products are retained: the product of the `R_k` and
the product of the `Q_k`. The raw product uses this fixed block grouping,
so its floating entries need not equal a monolithic pulse-by-pulse product.
The raw product and final corrected product must each pass the unchanged
`2e-10` full-phase target gate. The corrected product also passes the existing
final polar guard. The `1e-10` raw-defect and `5.1e-11` polar-correction guards apply to each
block and the product of corrected blocks. They do not bound the complete
raw-word defect or accumulated correction. The retained complete raw
defect reaches approximately `1.996e-10`, and the complete correction reaches
approximately `9.978e-11`. Both are reported numerical diagnostics, not new
certified error allowances. An incorrect unitary or global phase is not
repaired by these norm corrections.

For exact products of the represented matrices, set `E_0=0`, `r_0=1` and

```text
E_k = ||Q_k|| E_(k-1) + ||Q_k-R_k|| r_(k-1),
r_k = ||R_k|| r_(k-1).
```

Submultiplicativity and telescoping give
`||Q_k ... Q_1 - R_k ... R_1|| <= E_k`. Add the final polar correction norm
once to compare the final returned matrix with the raw product. The receipt
retains the evaluated expression, measured complete matrix change, sum and
maximum of block correction norms, and maximum block defects. These are
floating diagnostics: their evaluation does not enclose norm-computation or
matrix-product roundoff, and it is not added to the rational occupation
certificate as an independently certified numerical error. Initialization,
physical pulse precision and apparatus errors retain their separate scope.
